# Render locally, restore the costing sheet, add podcast last

The Three.js garment viewer now being built is the right way to show AYTO's outfits, and it should be the only visual tier that ships before the deadline: it runs on every drop with no key, no latency, no moderation and no faces, and it consumes exactly the `garment` and `print` fields the fashion writer now emits. A photoreal hero render through the user's own key is a cheap optional layer above it (**$0.03–0.05 per image** on FLUX.2 [pro] or Nano Banana 2.1, **$0.053** on GPT Image 2 medium, so under $0.30 per five-style drop), while a dressing simulator is the wrong feature: every commercial try-on API (FASHN **$0.075**, Kling **$0.07**, Google Vertex **$0.06**) needs a garment image plus a photo of a person, the research models are all non-commercial, and 3D CAD (CLO3D, Marvelous Designer, Browzwear) is enterprise-only, so none has a two-day path. What makes the fashion format a complete product for a small Indian maker is not the picture but the sheet: a per-style tech pack with blank points-of-measure rows, a costing sheet with a per-channel fee table that reflects the 2025–26 collapse of marketplace commissions (Flipkart **0%** on all fashion since July 2026, Myntra **0%** for eligible D2C brands since January 2026, Ajio still **15–25%**), the **₹2,500-per-piece GST cliff** (5% at or below, 18% above, since 22 September 2025), the four Legal Metrology declarations for loose garments, and a city allocation labelled as a taste lean rather than a demand forecast; all of this existed in commit 63a1f4a and is a restore-and-relabel job. Beyond film, reel and fashion, fifteen formats fit the engine; podcast concept (2–3 h, verified supply year), indie game pitch (3–4 h, the most non-obvious) and café menu-and-playlist (4–6 h, strongest city signal) rank first, but at most one should be added and only after the live URL, public repo, licence and Devpost page are green. The honest unfilled list starts with a working LLM key and the deploy, then the viewer's fallback and share-page snapshot, then the restored sheet, and only then any render or new format.

## The browser viewer is the right middle tier; renders cost cents, try-on costs faces

The decision already taken, a procedural garment on a mannequin drawn in Three.js with palette colours and a print derived from cues and no cloth physics, lands exactly where the research says the always-on visual should sit, and it supersedes the SVG croquis the outfit note proposed for day one. The fashion writer now returns, per style, a `garment` drawn from a fixed enum (`tee|oversized_tee|hoodie|sweatshirt|dress|kurta|jacket|shorts|set`) and a `print` object with `type`, `placement` and a `motif` of "2-4 words, no real brand or title", and its no-LLM fallback now ships two styles (a typographic-chest tee and a graphic-back hoodie) with a three-colour palette, so the viewer always has something to draw even while every LLM call still fails with a 401 (uncommitted diff to `src/writers/fashion.js`; status in `CLAUDE.md`). That schema is the viewer's contract, and it is the same vocabulary the commercial tools end in: Browzwear previews a style as "a 3D model or a schematic render" with its colourways, fabrics and trims, and exports an editable sheet rather than a locked picture ([Browzwear](https://browzwear.com/blog/vstitcher-and-lotta-update-techack-now-available-in-excel-format)), and Raspberry AI sells "3D Avatar to Photorealism" and "Multi-View Generation" with consistent front, back and side views as named features to Adidas, Levi's and Columbia ([raspberry.ai](https://www.raspberry.ai/)). A turntable mannequin that shows front and back of the same procedural garment is the small-maker version of that multi-view promise, with one property no image model can match: it cannot invent a seam. The same buyer's guide that lists Raspberry's features warns that generated garments "may contain impractical construction, inconsistent seams, inaccurate materials" ([Unite.AI](https://www.unite.ai/?p=341930)), which is the risk the procedural route avoids by construction.

Three constraints in the repo decide how the viewer should be wired, and each is a few hours rather than a redesign. First, the static share page sends `Content-Security-Policy: default-src 'none'; style-src 'unsafe-inline' …; img-src data:` (`src/pipeline/page.js`), so no script can run there; the viewer belongs in `public/app.js` only, and the share page should receive a PNG snapshot captured from the canvas (`toDataURL`) per style and embedded as a `data:` image, which the CSP already allows. That snapshot is also what the Devpost gallery and any screencast should show, because the rules make every screenshot binding: the project "must function as depicted in the video and/or expressed in the text description" ([Official Rules §4](https://qloo.devpost.com/rules)). Second, the viewer must degrade: when WebGL is unavailable or the library fails to load, the existing palette chips and style cards in `public/app.js` and `page.js` are the fallback, so a card is never empty; the earlier research's finding that judges spend "around five minutes" per project ([Jacklyn Biggin](https://dev.to/jacklyn/how-to-make-your-devpost-submissions-not-suck-37i0)) means a blank canvas costs more than a plain card. Third, the print layer is the one place the viewer can leak a name: `motif` is free text, and the hard rule that every name in the written output must trace to a Qloo request is enforced by the check stage on the written drop, so `print.motif` should pass through the same name filter, and typographic prints should render only the drop name or city, never a Qloo brand or artist string. The viewer's own honesty label is simple: "no cloth physics" means drape and fit are not shown, so the card should say "silhouette and colour, not fit", which keeps the receipts story intact.

Above the viewer, a photoreal hero render is optional and cheap, and it fits the BYOK model AYTO already has. The two keys most visitors hold are OpenAI and Google, and both are a single REST call: OpenAI's current models are `gpt-image-2.5-sunburst`, `gpt-image-2.5-flare` and `gpt-image-2`, with `gpt-image-1` "shut down October 23, 2026", so any older sample code is already broken ([OpenAI image generation guide](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/image-generation); [OpenAI deprecations](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/deprecations)); a 1024×1024 GPT Image 2 render costs **$0.006 low, $0.053 medium, $0.211 high**, and "complex prompts may take up to 2 minutes" ([OpenAI image generation guide](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/image-generation)). Google's Nano Banana 2.1 is **$0.0336 per 1K image**, has no free tier, and every output carries a SynthID watermark ([Gemini API pricing](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/pricing); [Gemini image generation guide](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/image-generation)); a vendor benchmark measured Nano Banana 2 at **15.5 s** and Pro at **30.8 s** per image ([Magic Hour](https://magichour.ai/blog/nano-banana-2-vs-nano-banana-pro-we-benchmarked-both-on-the-same-5-prompts)). FLUX.2 [pro] on fal is **$0.03 per 1 MP image** with a "Commercial use" tag ([fal FLUX.2 pro](https://fal.ai/models/fal-ai/flux-2-pro)) and is the only family that documents exact hex-colour prompting, with the instruction to tie each code to a named surface because unplaced codes "may produce inconsistent results" ([BFL hex colour guide](https://docs.bfl.ai/guides/usecases_t2i_hex_color_prompting)), which maps directly onto AYTO's `palette[].hex`. On quality, the Artificial Analysis arena ranks GPT Image 2.5 Sunburst at Elo **1222** ($210.7 per 1,000 images), Nano Banana 2.1 at **1176** ($33.6) and FLUX.2 [pro] at **1003** ($30) ([Artificial Analysis leaderboard](https://artificialanalysis.ai/image/leaderboard/text-to-image)), so Nano Banana 2.1 is the best quality per dollar for a BYOK default. At one render per hero style and five styles, a drop costs **$0.15 on FLUX.2 [pro], $0.17 on Nano Banana 2.1, $0.27 on GPT Image 2 medium and $1.06 at high**; precomputing the three showcases' heroes at boot on the project key is a one-time spend under a dollar, cached by `sha256(provider|model|style JSON)` in `.cache/`, and the project key should never render anything else. The prompt that keeps this safe is a flat-lay product shot ("white seamless background, top-down, no logos, no text, no mannequin, no person") with hex codes tied to surfaces; flat-lays avoid faces, bodies and skin tone, strip Qloo entity names from the image prompt even though they appear in the written drop, and if a model returns `moderation_blocked` ([OpenAI image generation guide](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/image-generation)) the card falls back to the viewer rather than retrying. Label every render "AI render" in the UI: Gemini images carry SynthID and Vertex adds C2PA, and the receipts story depends on saying which pixels are evidence and which are decoration.

The dressing simulator should be declined on the record, with reasons a judge can check. Every commercially usable try-on API is a second hop that consumes a garment image and a person image: FASHN moved to **$0.075 per image** on 1 March 2025 ([FASHN pricing update](https://fashn.ai/blog/pricing-update-for-developer-api)) and runs at about 7, 10 or 19 s by mode with flat-lay garment input supported ([FASHN v1.6](https://fashn.ai/blog/fashn-v1-6-our-best-virtual-try-on-model-yet-now-at-864-x-1296-resolution)); Kling Kolors v1.5 on fal is **$0.07** and requires `human_image_url` and `garment_image_url` ([fal Kling try-on](https://fal.ai/models/fal-ai/kling/v1-5/kolors-virtual-try-on/llms.txt)); Google's `virtual-try-on-001` went GA on 20 January 2026 at **$0.06 per output image** but needs a Google Cloud project rather than a single key ([Vertex Virtual Try-On docs](https://docs.cloud.google.com/vertex-ai/generative-ai/docs/models/imagen/virtual-try-on-preview-08-04?hl=en); [Vertex pricing](https://cloud.google.com/vertex-ai/generative-ai/pricing)). The open models that look free are not usable in a product: IDM-VTON's code and checkpoints are CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 ([IDM-VTON README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/yisol/IDM-VTON/main/README.md)), OOTDiffusion is "for NonCommercial purposes only" ([OOTDiffusion LICENSE](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/levihsu/OOTDiffusion/main/LICENSE)), CatVTON is `cc-by-nc-sa-4.0` ([CatVTON model card](https://huggingface.co/camenduru/CatVTON/blob/main/README.md)), and FASHN's own developer guide, a vendor with an interest in the conclusion, reports that the NC clause covers freemium apps and that one developer charging for a CatVTON app "received a cease-and-desist letter" ([FASHN developer guide](https://fashn.ai/blog/so-you-want-to-build-a-virtual-try-on-app-a-developers-guide-to-not-getting)). The 3D route beyond the browser is closed for a hackathon: CLO3D staff say "API support is only provided for Enterprise level" ([CLO support thread](https://support.clo3d.com/hc/en-us/community/posts/6876906333977-Clo3D-automation-using-API)), Marvelous Designer's Python API is "only offered in a new Enterprise-tier plan with floating licenses" ([CG Channel](https://www.cgchannel.com/2025/10/marvelous-designer-is-now-available-for-linux/)), Browzwear's open API is a partner programme ([Browzwear API](https://browzwear.com/api)), Google's consumer Doppl app shut down on 30 April 2026 with no API ([Photta](https://www.photta.app/pricing-and-reviews/doppl)), and Midjourney still has no official API. Try-on also imports the one risk the flat-lay and the mannequin avoid: Bloomberg's analysis of more than 5,000 Stable Diffusion images found high-paying job prompts "dominated by subjects with lighter skin tones" ([Marketplace](https://www.marketplace.org/shows/marketplace-tech/is-ai-more-biased-than-humans/)), and Levi's use of AI models "to increase diversity" drew a backlash the company had to walk back ([PetaPixel](https://petapixel.com/2023/03/29/levis-use-of-ai-to-increase-diversity-is-wildly-tone-deaf)); both are older (2023) but they are the precedents a judge will remember. If try-on is ever added, it belongs in a later tier fed by the hero render, on a fixed set of app-chosen mannequin photos spanning skin tones and sizes, never a user's or judge's photo.

## A Tiruppur unit quotes from a sheet, not a lookbook: restore 63a1f4a as editable assumptions

What a judge reads as "a complete product for a maker" rather than "a prompt output" is numbers with stated provenance, fields that recompute when edited, and an artefact that leaves the app. The commercial planning tools converge on the same panels: WGSN sells seasonal and colour forecasts with charts "to help you understand how to prioritise your assortment mix" ([WGSN Fashion](https://www.wgsn.com/en/products/fashion)), EDITED's assortment views flag products to "introduce, expand, reduce or discontinue" and build pricing architectures ([Techjockey](https://www.techjockey.com/detail/edited)), Heuritech attaches a visibility-growth number to each shape, colour, print and fabric ([CB Insights](https://www.cbinsights.com/compare/heuritech-vs-trendalytics)), and Raspberry AI's CEO now describes an agentic platform that runs from trend research through tech packs to assortment planning ([FashionUnited, Oct 2026](https://fashionunited.uk/news/business/raspberry-ai-ceo-cheryl-liu-ai-should-make-the-fashion-calendar-more-responsive-not-more-frantic/2026101490763)). AYTO's current writer produces the palette with its `from_tags`, the style list with hero flags, brands to study, first cities and channels; since the rebuild it lacks the per-style sheet, the costing table and an export. The audience that needs those is real and under-served: Tiruppur has about 20,000 units, nine in ten of them MSMEs ([Upstox](https://upstox.com/news/upstox-originals/investing/tiruppur-s-rise-how-one-textile-cluster-became-india-s-export-powerhouse/article-201077/)), and a TIFAC gap analysis found the cluster "has largely neglected fashion forecasting and trend analysis" ([TIFAC](https://tifac.org.in/index.php/reports-publications/recent-publications/2-uncategorised/1077-readymade-garment-cluster-fashion-design-technology-gap-analysis-,-tirupur,-tamilnadu)); the earlier research concluded that Tiruppur is the best story and the weakest buyer, so the sheet is there to make the showcase credible, not to sell to 20,000 units.

The tech pack has a known shape and a known boundary between what a model can draft and what only a sample can fill. Techpacker's guide lists six blocks: technical sketches with construction call-outs, a bill of materials (shell, trims, labels, hang tags, packaging), a points-of-measure table with tolerances, a grading sheet, a costing sheet and version history, and says the pack "may be updated several times during sampling" ([Techpacker](https://techpacker.com/blog/design/how-to-make-a-clothing-tech-pack)); its named POMs for a basic garment are full length, shoulder to shoulder, half chest, half hips, half waistband, full sleeve length, armhole, cuff width, neck width and neckband width ([Techpacker](https://techpacker.com/blog/design/how-to-make-a-clothing-tech-pack)). From a brief and Qloo's flavour tags, a language model can credibly draft the sketch brief, the BOM skeleton (composition, GSM, finish, thread, main, care and size labels, hang tag, polybag), print type with placement and approximate size, trims, the size set and fit notes, each labelled as a pre-sampling default. It must not print measured POMs, grading rules or supplier costs; a row reading "half chest 52 cm ±1" with no sample behind it is the one thing that lets a judge who knows garments call the output fabricated. The old stage at commit 63a1f4a asked the model for exactly the draftable fields (`fabric {composition, gsm, finish}`, `construction[]`, `print_or_embroidery {type, placement, size_cm}`, `trims[]`, `sizes[]`, `fit_notes`, `packaging`) and back-filled missing styles with 100% cotton, 180 GSM, bio-washed, screen print front chest 25×20 cm (`src/pipeline/stages-plan.js` at 63a1f4a). Restore it with four additions: a colourways column per style taken from the palette the writer already emits, a blank POM table pre-filled with Techpacker's names and a tolerance column labelled "fill after first sample", a size chart column in centimetres per size, and the label block described below. The size chart is not decoration: the 2022 Legal Metrology amendment requires sizes "in internationally recognised indicators (S, M, L, XL, XXL, XXXL) with metric measurement" ([S.S. Rana & Co.](https://ssrana.in/articles/mandatory-labelling-declarations-loose-garments-india/)), and Ajio makes size charts in centimetres "an explicit gate" at onboarding ([Base.com](https://base.com/en-IN/blog/?p=21070)). The export should be an editable sheet, which is what Browzwear's tech-pack export is (a single Excel file "meant to be edited before sharing") ([Browzwear](https://browzwear.com/blog/vstitcher-and-lotta-update-techack-now-available-in-excel-format)); for the two days left, a print-friendly per-style section on the existing static share page is the honest version of that.

The costing sheet is the cheapest high-value restore because it is arithmetic the user can change and watch recompute, and most of the old defaults survive contact with 2025–26 sources. The old `ASSUMPTIONS` object (MOQ 100 per style per colour, hero 300 and other 150 units, size curve S:M:L:XL:XXL = 1:2:3:2:1, unit cost fabric ₹120 + CMT ₹60 + print ₹30 + trims ₹20, logistics ₹60, markup 2.2/2.8/3.5× by tier, home-city share 0.2) was explicitly commented "Editable assumptions. Shown in the UI as assumptions, not facts" and merged with `run.input.assumptions` (`src/pipeline/stages-plan.js` at 63a1f4a). On MOQ, **100 pieces per style** is what an export-facing Indian manufacturer calls typical ([Optima Bags, Jul 2026](https://optimabags.com/blogs/optima-manufacturing/moq-for-custom-t-shirts-what-indian-manufacturers-actually-require)), Zyod advertises **50 per style** ([Zyod listing](https://startups.one.gob.es/companies/zyod)), while export-grade Tiruppur units ask **300 per colour per style** ([Fibre2Fashion, ESSA](https://www.fibre2fashion.com/garments/men-s-t-shirt-suppliers-22201491)) up to **2,000 pieces at FOB $2.67** for a 180 GSM round-neck ([Fibre2Fashion, Tirupur Hub](https://www.fibre2fashion.com/garments/men-s-cotton-round-neck-t-shirt-suppliers-25227972)); the fix is to expose "unit type" (small-batch job-work unit versus export unit) as the assumption that drives MOQ rather than a single number. On unit cost, a Tiruppur IndiaMART listing prices a 180 GSM bio-wash cotton tee at **₹190 with a 100-piece minimum** ([IndiaMART](https://www.indiamart.com/ganproduction/t-shirts.html)), so the old ₹230 landed figure sits inside the observed band and can return as a labelled default stamped "directory asking price, Oct 2026"; no dated primary Tiruppur price list exists, and the UI should say so. On size curves, no Indian norm exists, but the old 1:2:3:2:1 matches the bell-curve ratio packs consultants describe ([Vector Consulting](https://www.vectorconsulting.in/?p=11559)), so keep it with a one-line "bell-curve default, not demand data". On markup, the only chain found is US-oriented (retail roughly 4–6× cost including a wholesaler) ([AIMS360](https://aims360.com/fashion-business-resources/apparel-industry-pricing-margin-calculator-wholesale-retail-erp)), and Deloitte defines Indian mid-premium apparel as **₹3,500–7,000** ([Deloitte India](https://www.deloitte.com/in/en/about/press-room/accessible-premium-reshapes-indias-fashion-market.html)); the old tiers stay as assumptions with "no Indian benchmark found".

One old default is now wrong in both directions and must become a table: `marketplaceFeeShare: 0.18`. Flipkart launched 0% commission below ₹1,000 in November 2025 and in July 2026 removed the cap, extending **zero commission to all fashion at all price points** for about 90,000 sellers ([Outlook Business, Jul 2026](https://www.outlookbusiness.com/corporate/flipkart-scraps-seller-commission-across-all-fashion-price-points)); Amazon India expanded zero referral fees in March 2026 ([YourStory](https://yourstory.com/2026/03/amazon-india-expands-zero-referral-fees-125-crore-products-flipkarts-commission-waiver)); Myntra announced a **zero-commission model for emerging Made-in-India D2C brands on 9 January 2026** under Rising Stars ([Storyboard18](https://www.storyboard18.com/brand-marketing/myntra-unveils-zero-commission-marketplace-for-made-in-india-brands-87350.htm)), with the duration undisclosed and logistics, roughly 2% payment processing and advertising still charged ([StartupFeed](https://startupfeed.in/myntra-drops-commission-for-d2c-brands-under-rising-stars-heres-what-it-really-means-for-indian-founders/)); Meesho charges no commission in any category and lets sellers without a GSTIN register with an Enrolment ID for intra-state sales only ([WareIQ](https://wareiq.com/resources/blogs/sell-on-meesho/)); Ajio still takes **15–25% of net selling price** plus shipping and about 2% gateway ([RegisterKaro](https://www.registerkaro.in/guides/how-to-sell-on-ajio-in-india)). One vendor benchmark puts total deductions at 28–45% on Myntra/Ajio and calls returns "the biggest hidden fee" ([SW Cybernetics](https://swcybernetics.in/guides/india-marketplace-fee-benchmarks-2026)), which is the reason the table needs a return-rate column, not just a commission column. Instagram and WhatsApp are the first channel for most small labels (India had **413 million** monthly Instagram users in March 2025, with no reliable link between followers and revenue) ([Apparel Resources](https://apparelresources.com/business-news/retail/followed-indian-d2c-fashion-brands-instagram/)), and that row carries 0% commission, about 2% gateway and own shipping. The one hard pricing constraint the tool can compute exactly is tax: the 56th GST Council moved apparel above **₹2,500 per piece from 12% to 18%** and raised the 5% threshold from ₹1,000 to ₹2,500, effective 22 September 2025 ([EY India, Sep 2025](https://www.ey.com/content/dam/ey-unified-site/ey-com/en-in/alerts-hub/2025/09/gst-council-announces-major-rate-rationalization-and-trade-facilitation-measures.pdf); [Taxscan](https://www.taxscan.in/top-stories/key-highlights-of-the-56th-gst-council-meeting-rate-rationalization-trade-facilitation-1432230)), so an MRP of ₹2,499 versus ₹2,501 changes the tax inside the MRP by 13 points, and a warning when a computed MRP lands within ±₹200 of the line is a cheap, verifiable, India-specific touch. Whether the threshold is tested before or after tax is not settled by an official source; show it as "on the taxable value" with that caveat.

The compliance block needs one correction to the old text. The deleted line listed "maker name and address, country of origin, net quantity, month/year, MRP, consumer-care contact", which mixed two regimes. Since 1 January 2023, a garment sold loose or open, where the buyer can inspect it, is exempt from the full packaged-commodity rules on condition that the finished garment carries four declarations: maker's name and address (with country of origin for imports), consumer-care email and phone, size in S–XXXL with a metric measurement, and MRP inclusive of all taxes in rupees ([S.S. Rana & Co.](https://ssrana.in/articles/mandatory-labelling-declarations-loose-garments-india/)), and e-commerce listings must display the same declarations ([Lexplosion](https://lexplosion.in/garments-hosiery-products-sold-in-loose-or-open-form-are-exempted-from-compliances-under-legal-metrology-packaged-commodities-rules-subject-to-declaring-manufacturer-address-mrp-size-country-of/)). Only a garment that ships sealed in a polybag falls under the full Rule 6 list, which adds the generic name, net quantity and month and year of manufacture or packing ([Compliance Calendar](https://www.compliancecalendar.in/learn/lmpc-labeling-rules-mandatory-declarations-on-packaged-goods)). The restored checklist should show both lists behind a "sold loose" versus "ships sealed" toggle, add fibre composition as recommended practice rather than a cited mandate (the 1988 textile regulation's reach to finished garments could not be confirmed), and carry the registration dependencies as rows: GST registration is compulsory for any inter-state sale regardless of the ₹40 lakh threshold ([Tally Solutions](https://tallysolutions.com/gst/gst-limit-registration-threshold-india/)), Myntra treats a GSTIN as "non-negotiable", wants a trademark certificate or NOC, and takes 15–45 business days to approve ([GrowthJockey](https://www.growthjockey.com/blogs/myntra-seller-registration)), and Udyam is free, optional and useful for lending and delayed-payment protection but not a precondition to sell ([BimaKavach](https://www.bimakavach.com/blog/?p=4773)). That last point connects to the engine's own output: if `first_cities` crosses a state line, the checklist should say "this implies GST registration", which is the kind of consequence a prompt wrapper never draws.

The city allocation is worth restoring too, but renamed. The old stage weighted gap cities by gap score with a home-city share and produced a City, Market, Share, Units, Why table (`public/app.js` at 63a1f4a). The share is a Qloo-derived weight (lift beyond the country, cities that lean the same way) and is honest evidence; the unit count is share times an editable total. Present units as derived, keep the "Why" column pointing at the Qloo signal, and label the panel "starting split by taste lean, not a demand forecast", which also satisfies the kit's instruction to "distinguish a Qloo result from your own product interpretation" ([SAFE_USE.md](https://github.com/qloo/qloo-hackathon-kit/blob/main/docs/SAFE_USE.md)). Leave out, even though it is cheap: measured POMs, grading rules, named suppliers or sample prices (the only sampling figures are US benchmarks of $150–400 per pattern and $100–300 per prototype ([MakersRow](https://makersrow.com/blog/cost-of-manufacturing-clothes-price-quote/)) plus one Tiruppur listing's "Sampling: Free" ([Fibre2Fashion](https://www.fibre2fashion.com/garments/t-shirt-suppliers-12131693))), a kids'-set cost model (the only reliable datum is **₹119** for a single boys' tee ([IndiaMART](https://m.indiamart.com/srishanmugavadiveltex)), so show "tee cost + shorts cost", both editable), and any sell-through claim per city.

| Channel deductions | Deterministic per-channel table | Instagram+WhatsApp 0%/~2% PG; Meesho 0%; Flipkart fashion 0% (Jul 2026); Myntra 0% if Rising Stars else "verify"; Ajio 15–25% + ~2% | Replaces `marketplaceFeeShare: 0.18` |
| GST | Deterministic | 5% ≤ ₹2,500, 18% above (22 Sep 2025); cliff warning ±₹200 | New |
| Labels and registrations | Static checklist with toggle | Four loose-garment declarations; Rule 6 extras if sealed; GSTIN if inter-state; Myntra trademark/NOC | Corrects the old line |
| City allocation | Qloo lean × editable total | "Starting split by taste lean, not a demand forecast" | Yes, relabelled |

## Podcast, game and café fit the engine; add one, after deploy, or none

Fifteen further formats reuse the resolve → read → gap → audio → spots → audience → travels → write → check → diff → share pipeline with no new stage, and the cost of each is set by two things the code map makes explicit: whether supply can be counted (the gap stage counts entities with `properties.release_year >= thisYear − 4` inside the raw catalogue read, matched to a flavour by tag, in `src/engine/gap.js`) and whether a new entity type needs a fake-mode pool (`src/fake-qloo.js` falls back to `POOL.movie`, which would put "Sample Film" names into a podcast run). Qloo documents year filters only for book (`filter.publication_year`), movie and TV show (`filter.release_year`) and person ([Available parameters by entity type](https://docs.qloo.com/reference/available-parameters-by-entity-type)), and its `filter.type` enum covers artist, book, brand, movie, person, place, podcast, tv_show and videogame ([Insights API deep dive](https://docs.qloo.com/reference/insights-api-deep-dive)); a read-only probe run through the repo's own client on 28 October 2026, printing only counts and property keys, filled in what the docs leave out. Podcast and videogame carry `release_date` as an ISO string (8/8 raw entities, 20/20 recommend results each), book carries `publication_year` (8/8) but only 2/8 books carried tags, place carries no year but does carry `cuisine_description`, `specialty_dishes`, `price_level`, `live_music`, `event_spaces` and `good_for`, brand carries `products`, `price_range_usd_*`, `target_demographics_description` and `aesthetic_properties`, and `destination` carries nothing but `geocode` and no image. So one helper, `yearOf = release_year ?? publication_year ?? release_date.slice(0,4)`, makes supply countable for podcast and game; a book format should run demand-only; a café format must count supply as venues (`filter.type=urn:entity:place` + `filter.tags=<cuisine tag>` + `filter.location.query=<city>`), which is a genuinely new use of the gap formula. The same probe measured how much a city's top-20 differs from the country's: for Hyderabad versus India, place 19/20, tv_show 19/20, artist 18/20, person 17/20, movie 16/20, videogame 16/20, brand 14/20, book 12/20 and podcast only **5/20**; for Las Vegas versus the United States every type was 16/20 or higher. That one number reshapes the podcast format: in India the show list barely changes city to city, so the local angle must come from the tag lift and from the `person` column for guests, not from the show list.

Ranked by what each adds in a judge's five minutes against what it costs, three formats stand out, and the ranking also folds in the earlier research's casting localiser, which the new probe strengthens. The podcast concept is cheapest (**2–3 h**: yearOf helper, writer, UI renderer, `WITHOUT` prompt, fake pool) because its supply year is verified and its makers are the same creators who make reels and short films, so the home page stays coherent; demand is large (over **200,000 podcasts** were created on Spotify in India in 2023 alone ([Spotify newsroom](https://newsroom.spotify.com/tag/spotify-india-anniversary/)); **105 million** Indian listeners in 2024 while 82% of a ten-city survey were initially unaware of podcasts ([Storyboard18](https://www.storyboard18.com/brand-marketing/india-third-largest-market-for-podcast-listeners-globally-yet-only-12-percent-indians-currently-engaged-30314.htm)); about **436,000–605,000** shows active globally ([Barrett Media](https://barrettmedia.com/2025/10/13/number-of-active-podcasts-hits-new-high-in-september-2025-listen-notes-data-shows/))), and the incumbents are plain prompt wrappers (Talknotes returns six episode ideas from a description with CSV export ([Talknotes](https://talknotes.io/tools/idea-generator/podcast-ideas-generator))). The indie game pitch (**3–4 h**) scores highest on the "non-obvious" criterion: nobody expects a taste graph in game design, supply is countable, and the raw read's `steam_tags`, `gameplay_type`, `art_style`, `player_mode` and `platforms` give the writer checkable mechanics vocabulary; the pain is crisp (India has about **1,888 gaming companies** employing over 130,000 people ([Business Today](https://www.businesstoday.in/impact-feature/story/india-has-1900-game-studios-most-are-chasing-the-hardest-platform-543289-2026-07-16)); Steam saw **19,008 releases in 2025, nearly half with fewer than 10 reviews** ([GamingOnLinux](https://www.gamingonlinux.com/2025/12/over-19000-games-have-released-on-steam-in-2025-with-nearly-half-seeing-fewer-than-10-reviews/))), and the one weakness is images, only 4–8 of 20 games carried one, so the wall should borrow the movie and TV reference columns. The café menu and playlist (**4–6 h**) has the strongest measured city signal and the largest Indian maker pool (NRAI says it represents about **5 lakh restaurants** ([Tribune](https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/business/restaurant-sector-has-potential-to-provide-1-5-cr-jobs-nrai/amp)); Zomato averaged **3.06 lakh** active partner restaurants in Q3 FY25 ([Angel One](https://www.angelone.in/news/share-market/swiggy-vs-zomato-q3-fy25-financial-highlights))), it mirrors Qloo's own hospitality stories (Starbucks uses Qloo "to create in-store music playlists tailored to specific neighborhoods" ([Welcome to the Jungle](https://app.welcometothejungle.com/jobs/2YerO1Cd)); Michelin's taste matching since 2018 ([TechTarget](https://www.techtarget.com/ai/news/366570853/Taste-intelligence-and-AI-vendor-raises-25-million))), and its playlist half competes with Soundtrack Your Brand's AI generator serving over 80,000 businesses ([Music Business Worldwide](https://www.musicbusinessworldwide.com/b2b-music-streaming-service-soundtrack-your-brand-releases-an-ai-playlist-generator-for-business)), which is proof of demand so long as AYTO keeps its list as "artists this crowd favours beyond the country" rather than tracks; it is also the only one of the three that needs a new read, so it ships demand-only if the venue count misbehaves. Live event night (**2–3 h**) is the fallback when fewer than three hours remain: the audio, spots and audience stages already run for the reel and fashion plans, so hidden-gem artists as a booking list plus venues with `live_music` needs only a writer and a renderer, against **34,086** ticketed live events in 2025 ([Curly Tales](https://curlytales.com/india/trending/kantaras-l-repeat-watchers-to-k-live-events-inside-indias-entertainment-movement-in/amp/)) and 4,500 organised event companies ([Exchange4media](https://www.exchange4media.com/experiential-marketing-news/indias-experiential-industry-set-for-19-growth-in-fy25-samit-garg-144360.html)).

The rest of the catalogue is either too close to what exists, needs visuals the viewer does not provide, or points the read the wrong way. A wedding or festival theme (3–4 h) is fun and the season is enormous (CAIT projected **46 lakh weddings** between 1 November and 14 December 2025 ([CAIT](https://cait.in/wedding-season-2025-to-generate-₹6-5-lakh-crore-business-from-46-lakh-weddings-across-india-cait-delhi-alone-to-witness-₹1-8-lakh-crore-trade-from-4-8-lakh-weddings-indian/))), but the incumbents are venue-photo apps and a moodboard is expected. A book or web-novel pitch (2–3 h) has a huge pool (Pratilipi hosts over **15 million stories** ([Exchange4media](https://www.exchange4media.com/digital-news/jungle-ventures-announces-investment-in-pratilipi-142331.html))) but reads as the film format in different clothes and its supply count will mostly be zero. An out-of-home campaign (4–6 h) is Qloo's own JCDecaux case, which claims a **10× improvement in ad-targeting ROI** ([Qloo case study](https://qloo.com/blog/jcdecaux-case-study)), so it is proven rather than novel, and the incumbents (AdQuick's mock-ups onto real placements ([AdQuick](https://www.adquick.com/blog/visualize-your-brand-adquicks-ooh-creative-mockup-tool-2/))) are visual. Tourism (5–8 h) is the one format where the engine's direction is wrong: AYTO reads what a city's own crowd loves, a campaign needs which other cities' crowds lean to what this city has, which means inverting the `travels` heatmap into a new stage, and the buyer is weak (India cut its global tourism marketing budget to **INR 30 million** with no domestic line ([Skift](https://skift.com/2026/02/01/indias-travel-budget-small-marketing-spend-a-tax-win-infrastructure-push/))). Music single, YouTube series, retail assortment, merch collab, stand-up set, local magazine and theatre play all fit in 1–4 hours each but score low on novelty or carry unverified data risk (whether `person` returns working comedians was not probed). Visuals are essential only for OOH, wedding, tourism and magazine; podcast, game, café, event, book, music and series read well as text against the existing Qloo image wall.

| 2 | Indie game pitch | 3–4 | Yes (`release_date`) | 16/20, 19/20 | High; most non-obvious | yearOf, digest passes 4–5 properties, writer, UI, fake pool; borrow film images |

The honest schedule is one new format at most. Every new format also needs a `WITHOUT` prompt for the with/without card, a share-page body and a fake-mode pool, and a showcase in `src/presets.js` is the cheapest regression test because `test/pipeline.test.js` runs every showcase end to end. Given that no LLM call currently succeeds and the deploy, licence and Devpost page are pending, a new format is built only after those are green; if the choice is between podcast and game, podcast is the lower-risk build and game is the better story, and the casting localiser from the earlier report, which the new 85–100% `person` set-difference numbers make more attractive, is an in-format addition for the lead audience that competes for the same hours.

## Fourteen gaps, in the order they decide the score

What is unfilled sorts by what it costs if left undone against the four equally weighted criteria and the five disqualifying requirements the earlier report set out, with the deadline fixed at **30 October 11:45 pm EDT, which is 31 October 09:15 IST** ([Official Rules §1](https://qloo.devpost.com/rules)). Judges "may choose to judge based solely on the text description, images, and video" ([Official Rules §4](https://qloo.devpost.com/rules)), the organisers' test is "If your submission would work the same without Qloo, you're building the wrong thing" ([Overview](https://qloo.devpost.com/)), and the manager confirmed on 26 October that "Judges must be able to use the submission as a live tool/app" ([Devpost forum](https://qloo.devpost.com/forum_topics/45690-tasteatlas-no-cost-judging-access-and-cached-demo-requirements)). The first four items are eligibility and were already the earlier report's P0; the next four are what this research adds for the fashion format; the rest are optional in strict order.

## Conclusion

The research moves the fashion format's centre of gravity from the picture to the sheet. The viewer being built is the correct picture, because it is the only visual that is free, instant, faceless and incapable of hallucinating a seam, and the research's job is to fence it (fallback, snapshot, name filter) rather than to add tiers above it; the photoreal render is a cents-per-image luxury that can wait, and the dressing simulator is a feature whose every working implementation needs a human photo and whose every free implementation is unlicensed. What turns the drop into a product a Tiruppur unit could act on is the deterministic part that was deleted in the rebuild: a costing table whose defaults carry dates, a fee table that knows Flipkart and Myntra went to zero commission this year, a tax line that knows where ₹2,500 falls, and a label checklist that knows the difference between a garment sold loose and one sealed in a polybag. Those are arithmetic and static text, they recompute when the maker edits them, and they are the clearest evidence a judge will see that this is a product and not a prompt.

The format catalogue's most useful result is a negative one: the live probes show the podcast list barely changes between Hyderabad and India, which means the format most people would add first is the one whose city angle must be carried by guests and tag lift rather than by the show list, while the game and café formats, which nobody would add first, carry the strongest measured city signal. That pattern, that the engine's value is largest where the entity type is least obviously local, is the sentence to carry onto the Devpost page and into any finale conversation; it is also the reason to add nothing new until the live URL, the licence and the page exist, because the engine's depth is already its argument.
