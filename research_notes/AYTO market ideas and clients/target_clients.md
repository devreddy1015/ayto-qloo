# AYTO target clients: who is the main paying customer for city-level taste-gap briefs?

Scope note: AYTO takes "I'm making a crime short film in Hyderabad / a reel in Las Vegas / a kidswear drop in Tiruppur", reads city-level taste from Qloo's graph, finds the loved-but-under-served gap, and has an LLM write the idea into that gap. This note sizes four candidate segments, their current workflow and tool spend, their pain in their own words, and recommends which one is the main paying client. Research done 28 Oct 2026. Anything dated before 2024 is flagged "(older)". Numbers reported from secondary coverage of paywalled reports (FICCI-EY, Ormax, Kofluence) are flagged as such.

## Key Question 1: How many of each segment exist (India first, global second), and how fast are they growing?

### Takeaway
India releases ~2,000 films a year (1,972 in 2025, +8% YoY; Telugu 313, Tamil 315, Hindi 226), commissions ~274 streaming originals, and has roughly 4 million creators (up ~4x since 2020); Tiruppur has ~20,000 garment units (90% MSME). The film/OTT segment is small in count but large in per-decision money; creators are huge in count but tiny in per-head budget.

**Segment 4: Secondary candidates**
- Advertising/marketing agencies in India: a LinkedIn count of 40,275 agencies (Feb 2024, one practitioner's count); Infobel lists 145,188 registered "advertising & marketing" firms from MCA records (broad); a 2025 blog estimates 10,000+ digital marketing companies (unsourced) — [Source](https://www.storyboard18.com/quantum-brief/how-many-advertising-agencies-are-there-in-india-24196.htm); [Source](https://www.infobelpro.com/companies/india/advertising-and-marketing-agencies); [Source](https://pwskills.com/blog/how-many-digital-marketing-companies-in-india)

### Inferences
- With ~313 Telugu, ~315 Tamil and ~226 Hindi theatrical releases a year plus ~274 streaming originals, the Indian "greenlight decision" population is on the order of 2,000–2,500 decisions a year, made by (my estimate) a few hundred active production houses and a dozen OTT content teams. That is a small, nameable, reachable buyer list.
- The creator base (~4M India, 50–200M global) is 1,000x larger but, per Goldman and Adobe, mostly non-professional and low-income, which points to self-serve pricing, not sales-led contracts.
- Tiruppur's 20,000 units are mostly job-work suppliers to Western buyers (US+EU take the bulk of output); design intent largely sits with the buyer, not the unit, so the "what should I make" decision is only in play for the minority with own brands or domestic lines.

### Gaps
- No source gives a count of active production houses or OTT commissioning teams in Hyderabad, Chennai or Mumbai; I could only infer from release counts.
- No reliable 2025/2026 count of US creators (Adobe's 2025 survey does not restate the US number; the 2022 figure is older).
- No census of creator agencies or social media managers in India or the US.
- No reliable count of Indian fashion D2C brands; only market-size estimates that conflict (older $43.2B projection vs newer ~25%-of-D2C-GMV framing).
- No count of Hyderabad short-film makers; the only figure found is for Vijayawada and is older (2017).
- FICCI-EY film-segment revenue in rupees for 2025 was not visible in the coverage I could access (the EY PDF exceeded the fetch size limit).

## Key Question 2: What do they do today to decide what to make for a place, what does it cost, and what taste-intelligence tools do they already buy, at what price?

### Takeaway
Film/OTT teams in India buy post-greenlight tracking (Ormax Cinematix buzz/reach/appeal surveys; Parrot demand data) but there is no evidence of a pre-greenlight "what should we make for this market" product; creators buy $0–39/month SEO-style tools; agencies buy $20k–45k/yr Tubular-class intelligence; fashion forecasting (WGSN/EDITED/Heuritech) is custom-quoted enterprise pricing that Tiruppur MSMEs have "largely neglected".

### Cited Findings

**Film / OTT / production houses**
- Ormax Cinematix (OCX) surveys 2,000 theatre-going audiences every week on Buzz, Reach and Appeal for upcoming releases, combined with release scale, ticket price and holiday factors to forecast first-day box office (FBO); it supports "producers, distributors, studios, and exhibitors" on marketing, distribution planning and release scale; it reports tracking by "age, gender, markets and theatre types, enabling marketing & distribution prioritization"; Ormax states OCX "is not designed to answer more complex diagnostics-related questions related to a film's marketing campaign"; forecast accuracy "typically ranging between 75% and 85%" for opening day; a pan-India edition launched 2025 (Social Samosa, 2025) — [Source](https://www.socialsamosa.com/industry-updates/ormax-media-rolls-out-pan-india-edition-film-tracking-forecasting-tool-9070349); [Source](https://www.ormaxmedia.com/tags/ormax-cinematix.html)
- Ormax publishes monthly FBO-vs-actual accuracy updates (e.g., Jan 2026, Aug 2026) and sells annual/monthly Box Office Reports and the OTT Audience Report; no report or subscription prices are published — [Source](https://www.ormaxmedia.com/stories/ormax-cinematixs-fbo-accuracy-update-august-2026.html); [Source](https://mail.ormaxmedia.com/insights/stories/the-ormax-box-office-report-2025.html)
- Parrot Analytics does not publish pricing; prospective buyers must contact the company (Datarade listing) — [Source](https://datarade.ai/data-providers/parrot-analytics/profile). Its entry-level TV360 Monitor was offered at US$59/month for individual producers/creators (older, 2020) — [Source](https://www.pressreader.com/usa/the-mercury-news/20200713/282411286617353); [Source](https://todotvnews.com/en/parrot-analytics-presents-tv-analytics-platform-tv360/)
- How Telugu producers decide (older, Nov 2022): Dil Raju describes the producer model as "Let's set up a combination, spend money, sell it, and make some meagre profit"; from a ₹200 Cr Telugu gross, 18% (₹36 Cr) goes to GST and distributors take ₹110 Cr share, so "the producer gets only ₹85 crores finally into his hands"; the article frames the business as unprofitable ~85% of the time — [Source](https://www.gulte.com/movienews/213866/dil-raju-breaks-down-the-business-of-telugu-cinema)
- Budget logic (older, Aug 2022): on a Rs 10 budget producers used to expect ~Rs 6 from theatrical; post-pandemic they counted on ~Rs 5 from OTT and ~Rs 3 from theatres; talent day-rates rose from Rs 2 lakh to Rs 5 lakh — [Source](https://filmcompanion.in/features/the-ongoing-standstill-in-the-telugu-film-industry-explained)
- Film distributors use OCX market-level tracking for "distribution prioritization" (Social Samosa, 2025) — [Source](https://www.socialsamosa.com/industry-updates/ormax-media-rolls-out-pan-india-edition-film-tracking-forecasting-tool-9070349)

**Creators / agencies / social media managers**
- vidIQ pricing (third-party roundups, Jun–Aug 2026; vidIQ prices in AI credits and revises tiers often): Free $0 with 150 AI credits; Boost $39/month or $199/year (~$16.58/mo); Max ~$39/mo billed annually (sources conflict: ~$348/yr vs ~$468/yr); Coaching ~$99/mo; Enterprise custom — [Source](https://1of10.com/blog/vidiq-pricing/); [Source](https://outlierkit.com/resources/vidiq-pricing/); Capterra lists a $7.50/mo starting price — [Source](https://www.capterra.com/p/182133/vidIQ/)
- Tubular Labs: seat-based, custom quote; Vendr transaction data shows contracts from $20,000 to $45,000, averaging ~$27,000/year — [Source](https://vendr.com/buyer-guides/tubular-labs); [Source](https://tubularlabs.com/resources/tubular-labs-pricing-plans/)
- Trendpop "Starter" self-serve plan was $250/month with custom enterprise plans (older, 2022, Tubefilter); no current price found — [Source](https://www.tubefilter.com/?p=170384)
- Chartmetric (music/artist analytics): Artist $10/mo, Manager $60/mo, Premium $150/mo ($116.67/mo annual, ~$1,400/yr); Enterprise custom — [Source](https://us.fitgap.com/products/chartmetric); [Source](https://www.capterra.com/p/210527/Chartmetric/)

**Fashion / garment units**
- WGSN is an annual team subscription and offers "START by WGSN" for small fashion businesses; no price published — [Source](https://www.wgsn.com/en/demo). Older forum figures: ~$10K/year (2008, older) and "tens of thousands of dollars" for the full version — [Source](https://supertalk.superfuture.com/topic/29420-wgsn-how-much/)
- EDITED: contact-vendor pricing; AWS Marketplace says price depends on contract duration/terms; a reviewer describes pricing as "a premium that may be prohibitive for smaller retailers or brands with limited budgets" with "significant annual commitments" — [Source](https://www.capterra.com/p/173392/EDITED/); [Source](https://us.fitgap.com/products/edited)
- Heuritech: pricing page lists no figures (contact for tailored pricing); Heuritech was acquired by Luxurynsight in Nov 2024 — [Source](https://heuritech.com/pricing/); [Source](https://www.cbinsights.com/company/heuritech)
- A TIFAC gap analysis of the Tirupur readymade garment cluster, based on interviews with local entrepreneurs, associations and service providers, found the cluster "has largely neglected fashion forecasting and trend analysis", treating it as an untapped area — [Source](https://tifac.org.in/index.php/reports-publications/recent-publications/2-uncategorised/1077-readymade-garment-cluster-fashion-design-technology-gap-analysis-,-tirupur,-tamilnadu)
- The Apparel Export Promotion Council has run trend seminars in Tirupur for over a decade; the March 2019 edition (older) was aimed at micro and small knitwear units and run with WGSN, covering upcoming-season colours, silhouettes, prints and textiles — [Source](https://in.fashionnetwork.com/news/AEPC-to-hold-fashion-forecasting-event-in-Tirupur,1077646.html)
- TEA's 2021 programme (older) aimed "to elevate workers as designers and master craftsmen who can design fashionable wear of global standards" — [Source](https://www.dailypioneer.com/2021/india/tiruppur-to-become-men---s-fashion-capital.html)

**Secondary**
- Placer.ai (foot-traffic/location intelligence used by restaurants and retail): no public starting price, "Contact vendor" — [Source](https://www.capterra.com/p/199858/Placer-ai/)

### Inferences
- The Indian film tool stack is reactive: OCX tracks buzz for films that are already shot and announced. Nothing I found sells a pre-greenlight, city-level "what is loved but under-served" read, which is the slot AYTO occupies. Ormax is the obvious incumbent to compare against and the most likely channel partner or competitor.
- Price anchors: individual creators tolerate $5–39/month; agencies and media companies pay $20k–45k/yr for Tubular-class data; fashion forecasting is a five-figure annual commitment that Tiruppur MSMEs have historically accessed only through subsidised AEPC/WGSN seminars.
- For Tiruppur, the "deciding what to make" workflow is dominated by buyer tech-packs; the trend-analysis gap TIFAC identifies is real but the budget to close it sits with the ~100 large exporters, not the 20,000 units.

### Gaps
- Ormax does not publish Cinematix or report pricing; no third-party pricing data found.
- No current Parrot Analytics, WGSN, EDITED, Heuritech or Trendpop list prices; only older or anonymous figures.
- No evidence found of what a Telugu/Tamil production house spends on research or audience testing per film.
- No sources on how Indian creators or social media managers pick a city-specific hook today (I found burnout and content-fatigue data, but not workflow descriptions).

## Key Question 3: What is the concrete pain the "blank page + invisible local taste" framing addresses, in their own words?

### Takeaway
Producers say the business fails most of the time and that what counts as "commercial" changed under them; creators and social media managers report content fatigue and burnout at 46–77% rates; indie filmmakers say the audience question gets asked too late or not at all. The pain is documented; the "local taste" angle specifically is inferred rather than quoted.

### Cited Findings

**Film / OTT / production houses**
- Dil Raju (older, Nov 2022): "While the Producer has become the first person to spend, he will be the last person to get his money back." and "OTT platforms are not playing money in advance, but give you a paycheck only a month after the release of the film." — [Source](https://www.gulte.com/movienews/213866/dil-raju-breaks-down-the-business-of-telugu-cinema)
- Anonymous Telugu producer (older, Aug 2022): "If the OTTs are not paying the price we want or if they don't want to buy the film, we're stuck." Producer Srujan Yarabolu: "The so-called commercial films that were made a year or two ago are not considered commercial anymore." and what works now are "concept-based films which would earlier have been considered niche films." Exhibitor Sharat Marar: "We assumed that the audiences would all flock to the theatres once they opened." — [Source](https://filmcompanion.in/features/the-ongoing-standstill-in-the-telugu-film-industry-explained)
- Veteran producer C. Kalyan said Netflix, Amazon Prime Video, Disney+ Hotstar and ZEE5 have slowed acquisition of Telugu films (Deccan Chronicle) — [Source](https://deccanchronicle.com/tabloid/hyderabad-chronicle/telugu-producers-are-very-worried-1951873)
- Tollywood 2025 had "only six successful releases, while many big movies failed to recover their costs"; Telugu net ~Rs 1,922.77 crore; Game Changer, Hari Hara Veera Mallu and Akhanda 2 are listed among the big losers, attributed to "high budgets, long delays, and weak content" (Siasat, early 2026) — [Source](https://www.siasat.com/top-10-tollywood-films-that-suffered-heavy-losses-in-2025-3314267/)
- The 2025 winners were small, content-led films: Little Hearts at 1,223% ROI; Court: State vs A Nobody at 306% ROI on a Rs 10 crore budget; Sankranthiki Vasthunam collected Rs 186.9 crore on a Rs 50 crore budget (Siasat) — [Source](https://www.siasat.com/content-over-budget-4-tollywood-films-that-made-big-profits-in-2025-3306726/amp/)
- May 2026: a Telugu producers-vs-exhibitors standoff over revenue splits reached the South Indian Film Producer Council unresolved (Republic) — [Source](https://www.republicworld.com/entertainment/telugu-cinema/tollywood-crisis-explained-ram-charan-peddi-in-the-centre-of-telugu-producers-and-theatres-owners-standoff-2026-05-15-124342)
- Indie filmmakers (global): NYFA says filmmakers' anxiety over "What is the audience going to like?" can stall the creative process — [Source](https://www.nyfa.edu/film-school-blog/indie-filmmaking-moving-forward/); Indie Film Hustle: "Most filmmakers get so excited by the concept of a story... that they never ask the question" of whether the film has an audience — [Source](https://indiefilmhustle.com/?p=2384); producer Daren Smith (Sep 2026) argues filmmakers should test "whether its concept can galvanize a specific group of people versus assuming an audience will appear after the film is made" — [Source](https://www.utahbusiness.com/industry/2026/09/24/utah-film-producer-ditching-indie-playbook-daren-smith-brotherhood/)
- Funders' first question on indie projects is "who is the audience?" (The Greenlight List, No Film School) — [Source](https://nofilmschool.com/from-script-to-100k-equity-financing-navigating-the-greenlight-list)

**Creators / social media managers**
- Metricool survey of 1,500+ social media professionals (early 2025): 46% reported burnout or near-burnout, nearly half had considered leaving the industry, 69% report mental fatigue, 73% say they have "lost motivation or creativity" — [Source](https://www.bandt.com.au/things-have-intensified-social-media-experts-sound-burnout-alarm-as-report-finds-nearly-half-have-considered-quitting/); another write-up of the same survey cites 77% having experienced burnout in the past year — [Source](https://www.webpronews.com/the-social-media-manager-is-drowning-inside-an-industrys-burnout-crisis-and-the-ai-lifeline-that-may-not-be-enough/)
- Content fatigue in practitioners' words: "Being expected to constantly create original content that's able to somehow stand out from the sea of content already shared every day is a common source of frustration" (Socialinsider, 20+ experts) — [Source](https://www.socialinsider.io/blog/social-media-marketing-challenges)

## Key Question 4: Which segment has budget, urgency, and a short path from "idea" to "money"? Which are showcase demos rather than paying customers?

### Takeaway
Film/OTT production houses and their marketing/distribution arms (Hyderabad, Chennai, Mumbai) are the best main client: few hundred buyers, ₹10–200 Cr per decision, an admitted ~85% failure rate, a 2025 proof that concept-led films win, and an existing habit of paying Ormax for research. Ad and creator agencies are the second paying segment (they already pay $20k–45k/yr for Tubular-class tools). Individual creators and Tiruppur units are showcase demos.

Path: a brief per city per campaign is the deliverable they sell to clients. Risk: many agencies, fragmented, but SaaS-friendly.
  3. Social media managers of multi-location brands (restaurant/venue chains, retail) as a seat-based tier of (2); their pain (content fatigue, 46–77% burnout) is documented, their budgets are small but recurring.
  4. ## Key Question 5: What would each segment need the deliverable to be (brief, deck, script treatment, design pack, API) to act on it?

### Takeaway
Film teams act on a one-page treatment plus a pitch deck with market evidence (titles, logline, acts, comparable hits, city rationale); agencies act on a campaign brief per city; creators want a hook/audio/spots card they can shoot today; garment units need a design pack (silhouette, colour, print, size run) in buyer tech-pack vocabulary; distributors and chains want market tables or an API.

### Cited Findings
- Funders ask "who is the audience?" first and filmmakers find it costly to answer (The Greenlight List) — [Source](https://nofilmschool.com/from-script-to-100k-equity-financing-navigating-the-greenlight-list); Daren Smith's advice is to test whether a concept galvanizes a specific group before production — [Source](https://www.utahbusiness.com/industry/2026/09/24/utah-film-producer-ditching-indie-playbook-daren-smith-brotherhood/)
- OCX output is structured as Buzz/Reach/Appeal by segment plus a first-day forecast, i.e., producers and distributors are used to consuming audience data as tables by market, age, gender and theatre type — [Source](https://www.socialsamosa.com/industry-updates/ormax-media-rolls-out-pan-india-edition-film-tracking-forecasting-tool-9070349)
- Telugu production budgets are reasoned as a split of theatrical vs OTT revenue expectation (Rs 6 theatrical historically; Rs 5 OTT + Rs 3 theatrical post-pandemic), so any deliverable must speak to both buyers — [Source](https://filmcompanion.in/features/the-ongoing-standstill-in-the-telugu-film-industry-explained)
- Creators' tools are built around AI credits and per-video ideas (vidIQ Boost 2,000 AI credits; Max 6,000 credits and "Max Mode AI") — [Source](https://outlierkit.com/resources/vidiq-pricing/)
- Social media managers cite the demand for "fresh, engaging posts across multiple platforms" as "relentless", i.e., the unit they need is a ready-to-post idea, not a report — [Source](https://www.webpronews.com/the-social-media-manager-is-drowning-inside-an-industrys-burnout-crisis-and-the-ai-lifeline-that-may-not-be-enough/)
- Fashion forecasting is consumed as season-ahead colours, silhouettes, prints and textiles (AEPC/WGSN Tirupur seminar format) — [Source](https://in.fashionnetwork.com/news/AEPC-to-hold-fashion-forecasting-event-in-Tirupur,1077646.html); TIFAC frames the cluster's need as "forecasting, trend analysis, designing, and promotion of the developed designs" — [Source](https://tifac.org.in/index.php/reports-publications/recent-publications/2-uncategorised/1077-readymade-garment-cluster-fashion-design-technology-gap-analysis-,-tirupur,-tamilnadu)
- Agencies buying Tubular pay by seat for a team-wide intelligence platform — [Source](https://tubularlabs.com/resources/tubular-labs-pricing-plans/)

### Inferences
- Film/OTT (main client): a "greenlight pack" = one-page treatment (title options, logline, three acts, tone references drawn from what the city loves), a market-evidence page (which affinities are loved locally and under-served, comparable 2025 hits like Court/Little Hearts), and an exportable deck slide set for financiers and OTT buyers. Should also emit the OCX-style table by segment so it reads as familiar research. Pricing can be per project or per studio seat.
- Ad/creator agencies: a per-city campaign brief (insight, hook, audio, spots, creator shortlist criteria) exportable to the client-facing deck; seat-based team plan; optional API to batch cities for multi-city campaigns.
- Creators/SMMs (demo tier): a shoot-today card (hook, audio, three spots, caption angle) in-app; free or $10–30/month tier matching vidIQ norms.
- Tiruppur/D2C (demo tier): a design pack in tech-pack vocabulary (silhouette, colour story, print direction, size run, price band, local retail references); best delivered through AEPC/TEA programmes or a large exporter's design cell rather than to 20,000 units directly.
- Distributors, chains, labels: market-ranking tables and an API endpoint ("rank these 20 cities for this title/menu/tour").

### Gaps
- No source describes the exact artefacts Indian production houses circulate at greenlight (treatment vs "one-liner" vs bound script); the deck/treatment recommendation is inferred from OCX's table format and the funder-question evidence.
- No evidence of API demand from any segment; the API recommendation for distributors/chains is an inference from how Placer.ai and OCX are consumed.
