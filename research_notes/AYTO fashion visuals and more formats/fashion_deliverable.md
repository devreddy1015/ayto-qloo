# AYTO fashion deliverable: what a complete fashion-drop output looks like for a small Indian maker

Scope: the artefacts a Tiruppur knitwear unit, a Surat maker or a small Instagram label needs between "idea" and "units on sale"; what of that an AI tool can credibly produce from Qloo taste data plus a language model; how existing tools lay such a plan out; realistic 2025–26 numbers; and what separates "a complete product for a maker" from "a prompt output" in a judge's eyes. Researched 28 October 2026. India-first; older facts are marked with their date. Currency conversions from USD are mine at roughly ₹85 per dollar and are not sourced.

A note on source quality that applies throughout: almost no primary, dated, India-specific cost or fee schedule is published. Tiruppur prices come from B2B directory listings (asking prices, undated); marketplace fees come from seller-guide blogs that disagree with one another; the only primary sources found were the Legal Metrology amendment text (via two law-firm summaries), the GST Council's September 2025 decision (EY and Taxscan summaries), Techpacker's and Browzwear's own documentation, and news reports of Flipkart's and Myntra's commission changes. Where sources conflict I show both. Where only one vendor says something I call it a single anecdote.

## Key question 1: What fields does a standard tech pack contain, and which can be inferred from a design brief with stated assumptions?

### Takeaway
A factory-ready tech pack has six blocks: technical sketches with construction call-outs, a bill of materials, a points-of-measure table with tolerances, a grading sheet, a costing sheet, and version history. An AI tool can credibly draft the sketch brief, the BOM skeleton (composition, GSM, finish, trims, labels, packaging), print placement and the size set as labelled pre-sampling defaults; it cannot credibly produce measured POMs with tolerances, grading rules or supplier-specific costs, which need a block pattern and a sample.

### Cited Findings
- Techpacker's own guide lists the parts of a tech pack as technical sketches (front and back views and close-ups, with construction call-outs drawn on the sketch), a cover image, sizing information and units of measurement, Pantone colours and material placement, main fabric, lining, closure types and hardware; a Bill of Materials (shell, accent and lining material, thread and other trims, labels, hang tags, packaging supplies, supplier information "recommended, not required"); a measurements table; a grading sheet; a costing sheet; and version history with one-click PDF export. — [Techpacker, How to make a clothing tech pack](https://techpacker.com/blog/design/how-to-make-a-clothing-tech-pack)
- The points of measure Techpacker names for a basic garment are "Full length", "Shoulder to shoulder", "½ chest", "½ hips", "½ waistband", "Full sleeve length", "Armhole", "Waistband/cuffs width", "Neck width", "Neckband width", plus pocket width, length and opening; sample-size measurements are taken with the garment laid flat and each carries a tolerance (plus-or-minus margin). — [Techpacker](https://techpacker.com/blog/design/how-to-make-a-clothing-tech-pack)
- The grading sheet holds grading rules used to calculate the other sizes from the sample size; the costing sheet holds labour cost, material costs taken from the BOM, labels and tags, packaging, consumption and cost per unit with shipping and other additions. — [Techpacker](https://techpacker.com/blog/design/how-to-make-a-clothing-tech-pack)
- Techpacker says the tech pack should be "factory-ready", that front and back views and close-ups help the factory and pattern maker create the initial sample, and that the pack "may be updated several times during sampling". — [Techpacker](https://techpacker.com/blog/design/how-to-make-a-clothing-tech-pack)
- Techpacker's product documentation structures a pack as cards in three categories, Sketches, Materials and Measurements; each card holds files, a BOM table, supplier conversations and due dates, and the cards export as one PDF. — [Techpacker documentation](https://documentation.techpacker.com/techpack.html)
- Techpacker's ebook says a typical BOM covers main fabric, lining, thread, trims, closures, labels and packaging; measurement terms include body length, chest, waist, hips and armhole, with extra points for collars and pockets. — [Techpacker ebook, The Ultimate Guide to Tech Packs (PDF)](https://pages.techpacker.com/hubfs/Ebook%20for%20The%20Ultimate%20guide%20to%20Tech%20packs%20.pdf)
- A glossary definition adds style information, quality requirements and a revision history as standard tech-pack components. — [Hyperbots glossary, Tech pack](https://www.hyperbots.com/glossary/tech-pack)
- Browzwear's VStitcher/Lotta tech-pack export lets the user choose what goes in: a bill of materials covering colourways, fabrics and trims, or artwork with measurements and placement; the preview is a 3D model or a schematic render; output is a single Excel file (meant to be edited before sharing) or a set of folders (pattern pieces, BOM, artwork); custom templates are possible and saved settings can be reused. — [Browzwear blog](https://browzwear.com/blog/vstitcher-and-lotta-update-techack-now-available-in-excel-format); [Browzwear help](https://help.browzwear.com/hc/en-us/articles/4921683982489)
- Browzwear's Print to File outputs each 2D pattern piece full-scale with grain lines, style, shape and size names. — [Browzwear University](https://university.browzwear.com/vs-201-communications)
- Browzwear integrated Archroma's Color Atlas (4,320 colour references, draggable onto the 3D garment, specified in Archroma's format or RGB, carried into the tech pack) in 2020; whether the integration is still current was not confirmed. — [Just Style, 2020](https://www.just-style.com/news/browzwear-adds-archroma-colour-library-to-vstitcher-and-lotta/)
- Raspberry AI's CEO says designers can generate tech packs directly in the platform and send them to manufacturers, and that the product is shifting from image generation to an "agentic platform" that carries a collection from trend research and mood boards through tech packs, assortment planning and e-commerce assets (interview published 14 October 2026). — [FashionUnited, Oct 2026](https://fashionunited.uk/news/business/raspberry-ai-ceo-cheryl-liu-ai-should-make-the-fashion-calendar-more-responsive-not-more-frantic/2026101490763)
- Reviews of Raspberry AI list its features as sketch-to-render, fabric and trim visualisation, print development, image editing, team libraries, integrations and custom models, and warn that AI concepts are not automatically construction-ready or manufacturable, so teams should check the technical handoff before sampling. — [Unite.AI review (listicle)](https://www.unite.ai/?p=341979)
- An Indian manufacturer's blog sells a tech-pack design service "starting at $360" per style; it gives no sampling price or lead time. — [Optima Bags, 28 July 2026](https://optimabags.com/blogs/optima-manufacturing/moq-for-custom-t-shirts-what-indian-manufacturers-actually-require)
- US-market sampling benchmarks: pattern making typically $150–400 per style, a prototype $100–300 per piece, and most brands need two or three rounds; some manufacturers credit the sample fee toward the first order, many do not. These are not India figures. — [MakersRow, 2026](https://makersrow.com/blog/cost-of-manufacturing-clothes-price-quote/)
- One Tiruppur knitted-goods listing advertises "Sampling: Free", a 500-piece minimum, 60–90 day delivery and FOB $2 per piece (listing, undated, a marketing claim). — [Fibre2Fashion supplier listing](https://www.fibre2fashion.com/garments/t-shirt-suppliers-12131693)

### Inferences
- Of Techpacker's six blocks, a language model working from a design brief and Qloo's flavour tags can credibly draft: the sketch brief (silhouette, neckline, sleeve, hem, where the print sits), the BOM skeleton with explicit defaults (composition, GSM, finish, thread, main/care/size labels, hang tag, polybag), print or embroidery type with placement and approximate size, the trims list, the size set and fit notes, and packaging. Each of these is a "starting assumption the factory will revise at sampling", which is exactly how Techpacker describes the pack's life cycle.
- Three blocks should not be generated: measured POMs with tolerances (they come from a block pattern and a fitted sample), grading rules (same), and supplier-specific costing (it needs quotes). A tool that prints "½ chest 52 cm ±1" without a sample invites a judge who knows garments to call it fabricated. Show those rows as empty fields with the POM names pre-filled instead; that is what Techpacker and Browzwear do before a sample exists.
- Browzwear's export (BOM with colourways, artwork with placement, Excel meant to be edited) is the right model for AYTO's tech pack: an editable sheet per style, not a locked PDF. The old AYTO pack schema (fabric composition/GSM/finish, construction, print type/placement/size_cm, trims, sizes, fit notes, packaging) already maps onto the Sketches/Materials blocks; it lacked the colourway column and a blank POM table.
- Raspberry AI's own positioning (mood board → tech pack → assortment plan → e-commerce assets) is the closest commercial analogue to what AYTO's fashion format is trying to be; the difference AYTO can claim is that the brief is seeded by a city's measured taste rather than a prompt.

### Gaps
- No Tiruppur-specific sampling fee or lead time was found beyond one listing's "free sampling / 60–90 days"; the only numeric sampling costs are US figures (MakersRow).
- Cala (the design/production platform) returned no product results in any search; its current status and field layout are unknown.
- I did not obtain Techpacker's in-app field-by-field template; the blog and ebook give the blocks and POM names but not a canonical schema.

## Key question 2: What are current MOQ, cost, MRP and commission norms for small Indian apparel makers (cited and dated)?

### Takeaway
For a domestic small-batch first drop, 100 pieces per style is the most commonly quoted Indian MOQ, with newer B2B platforms advertising 50 and export-grade Tiruppur units asking 300 per colour per style up to 1,000–2,000 per style; a plain 180 GSM cotton tee lists at roughly ₹190–225 per piece at 100–2,000 MOQ and ₹375–400 for bio-washed or branded-quality at small MOQ. Marketplace commission has collapsed to zero on Flipkart (all fashion, July 2026), Meesho (all categories) and Myntra (eligible emerging D2C brands, January 2026), while Ajio still charges roughly 15–25%; shipping, returns and ~2% payment fees remain on every platform, and total deductions on Myntra/Ajio are quoted as 28–45% by one vendor benchmark.

Size curves
- A size curve is a percentage split applied to a total forecast; a US tee example is XS 5%, S 20%, M 35%, L 25%, XL 10%, XXL 5%, and retailers reallocate in-season from early sell-through. — [Racklify, What is a size curve](https://racklify.com/encyclopedia/what-is-a-size-curve-and-how-retailers-use-it/)

- Rule-of-thumb chain: wholesale is about 2–2.5x cost; retail is about 2.2–2.5x wholesale, so retail is roughly 4–6x cost (US-oriented guides). — [AIMS360](https://aims360.com/fashion-business-resources/apparel-industry-pricing-margin-calculator-wholesale-retail-erp); [Techpacker, Retail vs wholesale price](https://techpacker.com/blog/design/difference-between-retail-and-wholesale-price-in-the-apparel-industry/)

### Inferences
- The old AYTO default of 100 per style per colour is defensible as a domestic small-batch norm (Optima: 100 per style; IndiaMART Tiruppur listing: 100 minimum; Zyod/Fashinza: 50 per style). It is wrong for an export-grade Tiruppur unit, where 300 per colour per style (ESSA) to 1,000–2,000 per style (directory listings) is normal. The tool should expose "unit type" (small-batch job-work unit vs export unit) as the assumption that drives MOQ rather than a single number.
- The old unit-cost assumption (fabric ₹120 + CMT ₹60 + print ₹30 + trims/packing ₹20 = ₹230, plus ₹60 logistics) sits inside the observed band for a plain 180 GSM tee (₹190–225 at 100–2,000 MOQ; ₹375–400 for bio-washed/branded quality at small MOQ; ₹340–510 for export-facing custom prints), so restoring it as a labelled editable default is reasonable. Fabric at ₹120 per tee is on the high side against ₹324–356/kg knit at ~0.2–0.25 kg per tee (₹65–90); the sources for that are undated, so keep it editable rather than "correct" it.
- A single marketplaceFeeShare of 0.18 is now wrong in both directions: Flipkart fashion, Meesho, and Myntra-for-eligible-brands are at 0% commission, Ajio is 15–25%, and non-commission deductions (shipping, returns/RTO, ~2% payment gateway, ads) are what remain. The economics block should carry a per-channel table (commission %, shipping per unit, return rate, PG %) with editable defaults and the date each default was taken.
- The old markup tiers (value 2.2x, mid 2.8x, premium 3.5x on landed cost) are below the US wholesale-plus-retail chain (4–6x) but that chain includes a wholesaler; for a D2C maker selling direct, 2.5–3.5x landed is the commonly repeated rule of thumb and no India-specific figure was found, so keep them as assumptions with the note "no Indian benchmark found".
- The ₹2,500-per-piece GST cliff (5% at or below, 18% above, see Q3) is the one hard pricing constraint the tool can compute exactly: an MRP of ₹2,499 versus ₹2,501 changes the tax inside the MRP by 13 points. Showing that cliff in the costing sheet is a cheap, verifiable, India-specific touch.
- For size curves no Indian norm exists in the sources; the old 1:2:3:2:1 (S–XXL) is consistent with the bell-curve ratio packs cited, so keep it editable with a one-line note that it is a bell-curve default, not demand data.
- Kids' set costing cannot be grounded: the only reliable Tiruppur datum is ₹119 for a single boys' tee; a set default should be shown as "tee cost + shorts cost" with both editable.

### Gaps
- No dated, primary Tiruppur price list for a 180 GSM tee exists online; all figures are asking prices from directories. The tool should say so in the UI ("directory asking prices, Oct 2026").
- No reliable per-set price or MOQ for a kids' tee-and-shorts set.
- Myntra's and Ajio's own commission schedules are not public; all percentages are from seller-service vendors who disagree.
- No published stall fees for the named Indian fashion flea markets (Lil Flea, Sunday Soul Sante).
- No Indian cost-to-MRP multiplier by tier; no Indian size-curve data.
- Flipkart's post-July-2026 fee card for non-commission charges (shipping, collection, fixed fees) was not retrieved.

## Key question 3: What Indian labelling and compliance rules apply to a first drop (Legal Metrology declarations, GST thresholds, Udyam)?

### Takeaway
A garment sold loose (customer can inspect it) needs four declarations on the finished garment, and the same four online: maker's name and address with country of origin, consumer-care email and phone, size in S–XXXL with metric measurement, and MRP inclusive of all taxes in rupees (Legal Metrology (Packaged Commodities) Third Amendment Rules 2022, in force 1 January 2023). A pre-packed garment (sealed polybag, not inspectable) falls under the full Rule 6 list, adding generic name, net quantity and month/year of manufacture or packing. Apparel GST is 5% at or below ₹2,500 per piece and 18% above, since 22 September 2025; GST registration is mandatory for any inter-state sale and for Myntra/Ajio regardless of turnover, while Meesho allows an Enrolment ID for intra-state sales; Udyam is free and optional.

### Cited Findings
- The Legal Metrology (Packaged Commodities) Third Amendment Rules 2022 were notified on 22 August 2022 and took effect on 1 January 2023; they insert a clause exempting garments or hosiery sold loose or open (so the consumer can inspect before buying) from Rule 6 declarations, on condition that the finished garment carries: (1) name and address of the manufacturer, marketer, brand owner or importer, with country of origin for imports; (2) consumer-care email ID and phone number; (3) sizes in internationally recognised indicators (S, M, L, XL, XXL, XXXL) with metric measurement in cm or m; (4) maximum retail price inclusive of all taxes in Indian currency. "If the above information is mentioned on the finished loose garment goods, only then the exemption" applies. — [S.S. Rana & Co.](https://ssrana.in/articles/mandatory-labelling-declarations-loose-garments-india/)
- The exemption applies only to sale of finished products, and e-commerce websites selling these garments must display the same declarations on the website. — [Lexplosion](https://lexplosion.in/garments-hosiery-products-sold-in-loose-or-open-form-are-exempted-from-compliances-under-legal-metrology-packaged-commodities-rules-subject-to-declaring-manufacturer-address-mrp-size-country-of/)
- Full Rule 6 declarations for a pre-packed commodity: name and address of the manufacturer, packer or importer; country of origin (imports); common or generic name; net quantity in a standard unit; month and year of manufacture, packing or import; best-before date where applicable; MRP inclusive of all taxes; consumer-care name, address, telephone and email; size or dimensions where size is an important characteristic; unit sale price where applicable; online listings should generally reproduce the mandatory declarations. — [Compliance Calendar, LMPC labelling rules](https://www.compliancecalendar.in/learn/lmpc-labeling-rules-mandatory-declarations-on-packaged-goods)
- The base text of the Legal Metrology (Packaged Commodities) Rules 2011 is available as a state-government PDF. — [Nagaland Legal Metrology, 2011 Rules (PDF)](https://legalmetrology.nagaland.gov.in/documents/11/9_The_Legal_Metrology_Package_Commodities_Rules_2011.pdf)
- Two 2025 amendments to the Packaged Commodities Rules were found, neither touching garments: the 2025 amendment aligning medical-device packages with the Medical Devices Rules 2017, and the Second Amendment Rules 2025 (G.S.R. 881(E), 2 December 2025, in force 1 February 2026) adding a pan-masala proviso to Rule 26(a). — [Drishti IAS summary](https://www.drishtiias.com/daily-updates/daily-news-analysis/legal-metrology-packaged-commodities-amendment-rules-2025/print_manually); [TeamLease RegTech](https://teamleaseregtech.com/updates/article/50220/legal-metrology-packaged-commodities-second-amendment-rules-2025/)
- Textiles (Consumer Protection) Regulation 1988 (older, status unverified): a Lok Sabha answer from 2000 says finished fabric must carry the manufacturer's name and address, fibre composition, and month and year of packing, with fibre composition stamped on alternate metres of selvedge; grey cloth before processing is exempt; the Regulation does not provide for price stamping. These answers date from 2000 and I could not confirm the Regulation's current status or whether it reaches finished garments. — [Lok Sabha answer, 2000 (PDF)](https://eparlib.nic.in/bitstream/123456789/427737/1/4627.pdf)
- GST on apparel: the 56th GST Council meeting removed the 12% slab; articles of apparel and clothing accessories with a sale value exceeding ₹2,500 per piece moved from 12% to 18%, and the threshold for the concessional 5% rate rose from ₹1,000 to ₹2,500 per piece; the changes took effect on 22 September 2025. — [EY India tax alert, Sep 2025 (PDF)](https://www.ey.com/content/dam/ey-unified-site/ey-com/en-in/alerts-hub/2025/09/gst-council-announces-major-rate-rationalization-and-trade-facilitation-measures.pdf); [Taxscan](https://www.taxscan.in/top-stories/key-highlights-of-the-56th-gst-council-meeting-rate-rationalization-trade-facilitation-1432230)
- Handmade/hand-embroidered shawls stay at 5% with the threshold removed. — [Taxscan](https://www.taxscan.in/top-stories/key-highlights-of-the-56th-gst-council-meeting-rate-rationalization-trade-facilitation-1432230)
- Whether the ₹2,500 test is on the pre-tax value: forum commentary says the threshold is exclusive of the tax element; this is informal, not official guidance. — [TaxTMI forum](https://www.taxtmi.com/forum/issue?id=120496)
- An Indian retail-software blog says the rate is tested on the discounted invoice price (a ₹2,999 kurta discounted 20% to ₹2,399 is taxed at 5%) and that MRP already includes GST. — [Petpooja festive planning guide](https://blog.petpooja.com/templates-checklist/clothing-retail-seasonal-planning-guide/); [Petpooja pricing template](https://blog.petpooja.com/templates-checklist/product-pricing-calculator-template/)
- GST registration threshold for goods is ₹40 lakh in normal-category states (₹20 lakh in special-category states), only where the state opted in, and any inter-state taxable supply of goods makes registration compulsory regardless of turnover. — [Tally Solutions](https://tallysolutions.com/gst/gst-limit-registration-threshold-india/)
- Intra-state suppliers of goods through e-commerce portals need not register if turnover is below ₹40 lakh, effective 1 January 2023 (secondary report; older guides still say e-commerce sellers must register at any turnover). — [Outlook Business](https://www.outlookbusiness.com/news/gst-on-goods-gst-registration-exemption-for-online-seller-with-low-turnover-to-boost-e-commerce-news-205717); contradicted by [Tax2win guide](https://tax2win.in/guide/gst-registration-for-ecommerce)
- Platform practice overrides the legal threshold: Myntra and Ajio require a (regular, non-composition) GSTIN; Meesho allows an Enrolment ID/UIN limited to intra-state sales for turnover under ₹40 lakh. — [GrowthJockey (Myntra)](https://www.growthjockey.com/blogs/myntra-seller-registration); [Shiprocket (Ajio)](https://www.shiprocket.in/blog/ajio-seller-guide/); [WareIQ (Meesho)](https://wareiq.com/resources/blogs/sell-on-meesho/)
- Udyam (MSME) registration is optional, free, and valid for life; it is practically useful for priority-sector lending and delayed-payment protection but not a legal precondition to sell. — [BimaKavach guide](https://www.bimakavach.com/blog/?p=4773)
- Myntra lists "Udyog Aadhaar"/MSME details among documents MSME sellers may need, and a trademark certificate (or NOC/brand authorisation for resellers). — [Ginesys](https://www.ginesys.in/blog/step-by-step-guide-to-myntra-onboarding-for-fashion-retailers)

### Inferences
- The old AYTO line "Each pack needs Legal Metrology declarations: maker name and address, country of origin, net quantity, month/year, MRP, consumer-care contact" mixed the two regimes. For a drop sold loose or on Instagram/WhatsApp with try-before-buy, the correct four-item list is the 2022 amendment's; "net quantity" and "month/year" only apply if the garment ships sealed in a polybag as a pre-packed commodity. A restored label checklist should show both lists and let the maker tick "sold loose" vs "pre-packed".
- The size declaration rule (S–XXXL plus cm) and Ajio's size-chart gate point the same way: a size chart in centimetres per fit is both a legal and a marketplace requirement, so the tech pack's size set should carry a cm chart column even if the POM rows are blank.
- Fibre composition on the label is good practice and may be required by the 1988 textile regulation for fabric, but I could not confirm it binds finished garments today; the tool should include "fibre composition" on the main label as a recommended declaration, not a cited legal mandate.
- For channel planning: a maker with an Enrolment ID can start on Meesho and Instagram/WhatsApp within their state with no GSTIN; Myntra and Ajio require GSTIN plus trademark or NOC and weeks of approval, so "first cities" that cross state lines imply GST registration. That dependency is cheap to surface as a checklist row.

### Gaps
- I did not read the gazette text of the 2022 amendment or of Rule 6(10) on e-commerce display; both findings rest on law-firm summaries.
- Current status of the Textiles (Consumer Protection) Regulation 1988 for finished garments is unverified (only Lok Sabha answers from 2000 found).
- Whether the ₹2,500 GST threshold is tested before or after tax is not settled by an official source in these results.
- The e-commerce intra-state GST exemption (1 January 2023) was not confirmed against a CBIC notification.

## Key question 4: How do fashion planning tools lay out a collection plan on screen (what a judge expects to see)?

### Takeaway
Commercial tools present a plan as a small set of recurring panels: a colour story with named swatches, a trend/attribute view with a growth or share number, an assortment grid (styles x colourways x price points, with introduce/expand/reduce flags), a per-style tech-pack sheet (sketch, BOM, placement, measurements), and a costing/pricing architecture table. A judge who has seen WGSN, EDITED, Heuritech, Browzwear or Raspberry AI will look for exactly those panels, each tied to an evidence source and each editable or exportable; prose alone reads as a prompt output.

### Cited Findings
- WGSN Fashion's product page lists Seasonal and Colour Forecasts, Big Ideas, Buyers' Briefing, Sustainability and Innovation, and TrendCurve analysis, with "data-driven charts and AI driven forecasts to help you understand how to prioritise your assortment mix and product phasing". — [WGSN Fashion](https://www.wgsn.com/en/products/fashion)
- A University of Delaware thesis comparing 20 paired WGSN and EDITED womenswear forecasts (S/S 2018) found the two produced very similar colour forecasts and least-similar design-detail forecasts. — [UDel thesis](https://udspace.udel.edu/handle/19716/28200)
- EDITED is described as covering pre-season planning to in-season trading in three areas, assortment, pricing and site merchandising; tools to build pricing architectures and optimise promotions; assortment views that identify products to introduce, expand, reduce or discontinue, plus overstocked, underperforming and high-potential products; customisable dashboards combining market, competitor and own-business metrics; competitor benchmarking of products, pricing, assortments and promotions. — [Techjockey, EDITED](https://www.techjockey.com/detail/edited); [SoftwareFinder, EDITED](https://softwarefinder.com/retail/edited); [GetApp, EDITED](https://www.getapp.co.uk/software/2079705/edited)
- Heuritech lets brands analyse the predicted visibility growth of shapes, colours, prints and fabrics and their forecasted market share, from image recognition over millions of social images; it was acquired by Luxurynsight in November 2024. — [CB Insights, Heuritech vs Trendalytics](https://www.cbinsights.com/compare/heuritech-vs-trendalytics); [Heuritech FAQ on its forecasting model](https://faq.heuritech.com/en/articles/9115571-forecasting-breakthrough)
- A Heuritech x Pantone colour report (Fall 2020) presented five named palettes (Mint, Pastel, Earth Tones, Blue Tones, Pink) derived from social-media image recognition; it is a colour-only report. — [Heuritech market report](https://heuritech.com/market-reports/pantone-fashion-colors-fall-2020/)
- Raspberry AI (as of October 2026) is positioned as an agentic platform running from trend research and mood boards through tech packs, assortment planning and e-commerce assets, and in September 2026 partnered with the Pantone Color Institute around the Spring/Summer 2027 Fashion Color Trend Report. — [FashionUnited, Oct 2026](https://fashionunited.uk/news/business/raspberry-ai-ceo-cheryl-liu-ai-should-make-the-fashion-calendar-more-responsive-not-more-frantic/2026101490763)
- Browzwear shows a style as a 3D model or schematic render with a BOM covering colourways, fabrics and trims and artwork with measurements and placement, exported as an editable Excel sheet or a folder set. — [Browzwear blog](https://browzwear.com/blog/vstitcher-and-lotta-update-techack-now-available-in-excel-format)
- Techpacker shows a pack as cards grouped into Sketches, Materials and Measurements, exportable as a single PDF. — [Techpacker documentation](https://documentation.techpacker.com/techpack.html)
- Hackathon rubric examples (not Qloo's): an Okta hackathon used a pass/fail viability gate then three equally weighted criteria, creativity and originality, implementation, potential impact; another event's judge guide used originality, execution, applicability, presentation and learning. — [Okta developer forum](https://devforum.okta.com/t/what-are-the-judging-criteria-for-the-hackathon/15842)

### Inferences
- The screen vocabulary a judge expects, distilled from these tools: (1) a palette strip with named swatches and the evidence each came from (WGSN/Heuritech colour reports), (2) an attribute or trend list with a number beside each (Heuritech's visibility growth; in AYTO's case the Qloo lift beyond the country), (3) an assortment grid of styles x colourways x price point with hero flags (EDITED's introduce/expand/reduce), (4) a per-style sheet with sketch brief, BOM, print placement and size set (Techpacker/Browzwear), (5) a pricing architecture table showing cost, MRP, channel deductions and margin (EDITED's pricing architecture), and (6) an export (PDF/Excel) because every one of these tools ends in a file handed to someone else.
- AYTO's current fashion writer already produces (1), (2) in part (from_tags per style), and a flat version of (3); it lacks (4), (5) and (6) since the rebuild. Restoring (4) and (5) as deterministic, editable panels and adding a print-friendly export on the existing static share page would cover what the commercial tools show, at the small-maker scale.
- "Complete product" versus "prompt output" in a judge's eyes is mostly about three properties the cited tools share: numbers with stated provenance and dates, fields the user can edit and see recompute, and an artefact that leaves the app (a sheet a Tiruppur unit could quote from). Prose positioning, a list of styles and a palette without those three reads as generated text.

### Gaps
- Qloo's own hackathon judging criteria could not be retrieved (the Devpost page returned HTTP 403); the rubric examples above are from other events.
- No screenshots or sample reports of WGSN, EDITED or Heuritech dashboards were obtainable; layouts are inferred from product descriptions and review-site feature lists.
- Cala's product layout is unknown (no results).
