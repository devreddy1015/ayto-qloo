# Hyderabad producers pay, creators demo, ship Friday

AYTO's main client should be the film and OTT production house, starting in Hyderabad: it is a small, nameable buyer list (India released **1,972 films in 2025**, 313 of them Telugu, plus 274 streaming originals), it spends ₹10–200 Cr per greenlight, it has just lived through a year in which **only six Telugu releases succeeded** while a ₹10 Cr concept film (*Court*) returned **306%**, its producers say on record that they no longer know what "commercial" means, and it already pays Ormax for audience research that only starts *after* a film is announced. Individual creators (4 million in India, half earning under $15k a year) and Tiruppur garment units (20,000 units, 90% MSME, a cluster that has "largely neglected" trend analysis) are the right showcase demos and a self-serve funnel, not the anchor customer; ad and creator agencies, which already pay $20k–45k a year for Tubular-class intelligence, are the second paying segment. On the product side, no commercial tool starts from a place and a format, reads what that city loves beyond its own country, scores what is under-made, and writes the creative: incumbents are country-level demand trackers (Parrot, Ormax), script scorers (Cinelytic, Largo, Vault), enterprise trend subscriptions (WGSN), or writers with no taste data (Jasper, Sudowrite, Final Draft). The strongest extensions keep that inversion (place → idea) and add one Qloo primitive each: a casting localiser on the `person` entity type, a two-city port on `/v2/analysis/compare`, and a multi-city ad-creative localiser; site selection, venue booking, tour routing, sponsor matching and due diligence are different products already occupied by TasteRouter, Open Slot, Advance, SponsorMatch and 2025 winner Alloy. For the hackathon, five hard items decide eligibility (a live public demo with no login, a public repo with a root licence visible in GitHub's About box, a text description that says what is "Qloo-powered", external hosting, and a working Qloo-integrated agentic app), four equally weighted criteria decide the score, and ties break on depth of Qloo use. The deadline is **Fri 30 Oct 11:45 pm EDT = Sat 31 Oct 09:15 IST**; the next 48 hours should go, in order, to the live URL, the Devpost page, visible Qloo evidence with the with/without card, a clean-clone README, and only then an optional video or one extension.

## Hyderabad film and OTT producers are the one client worth naming

The film segment is small in headcount but large in money per decision, which is exactly the shape a pre-greenlight tool needs. India released 1,972 films in 2025, up 8% on 2024, with Tamil at 315, Telugu at 313, Kannada at 267 and Hindi at 226; domestic theatrical made up 63% of film income while OTT rights fell to 8% ([Bollywood Hungama, FICCI-EY 2026](https://www.bollywoodhungama.com/?p=2114501)). Streaming originals fell 13% to **274 titles** in 2025, yet Telugu originals rose from 28 to 35 while Hindi's share slid to 60% ([ContentAsia, Ormax](https://www.contentasia.tv/news/indian-streaming-originals-plummet-ormax-media-tracks-five-year-decline-2025-lows)), and regional languages' share of OTT consumption climbed from 27% in 2020 to 56% in 2025 ([EY](https://www.ey.com/content/dam/ey-unified-site/ey-com/en-in/insights/media-entertainment/documents/ey-stories-scale-and-impact-unlocking-indias-media-and-entertainment-economy.pdf)). Taken together, the Indian "greenlight decision" population is on the order of 2,000–2,500 a year, made by a few hundred active production houses and a dozen OTT content teams: a list you can name, email and visit, which is not true of four million creators.

The urgency is on the record in producers' own words. Tollywood 2025 had "only six successful releases, while many big movies failed to recover their costs", with *Game Changer* and *Hari Hara Veera Mallu* among the losers, blamed on "high budgets, long delays, and weak content" ([Siasat](https://www.siasat.com/top-10-tollywood-films-that-suffered-heavy-losses-in-2025-3314267/)). The winners were small and concept-led: *Little Hearts* at **1,223% ROI**, *Court: State vs A Nobody* at **306% on a ₹10 Cr budget**, *Sankranthiki Vasthunam* collecting ₹186.9 Cr on ₹50 Cr ([Siasat](https://www.siasat.com/content-over-budget-4-tollywood-films-that-made-big-profits-in-2025-3306726/amp/)). Producer Srujan Yarabolu put the confusion plainly: "The so-called commercial films that were made a year or two ago are not considered commercial anymore", and what works now are "concept-based films which would earlier have been considered niche films" ([Film Companion](https://filmcompanion.in/features/the-ongoing-standstill-in-the-telugu-film-industry-explained)). Dil Raju framed the economics: the producer "has become the first person to spend" and "will be the last person to get his money back" ([Gulte](https://www.gulte.com/movienews/213866/dil-raju-breaks-down-the-business-of-telugu-cinema)), and veteran producer C. Kalyan said Netflix, Prime Video, Hotstar and ZEE5 have slowed Telugu acquisitions ([Deccan Chronicle](https://deccanchronicle.com/tabloid/hyderabad-chronicle/telugu-producers-are-very-worried-1951873)). Markets have also stopped transferring: Ormax reports the dubbed-South share of Hindi collections collapsed from 31% to 7% in 2025 even as 37 films crossed ₹100 Cr ([Ormax Box Office Report 2025](https://mail.ormaxmedia.com/insights/stories/the-ormax-box-office-report-2025.html)), which is the commercial version of AYTO's thesis that Hyderabad's taste is not Mumbai's.

Crucially, this buyer already pays for research, and the incumbent leaves AYTO's slot empty. Ormax Cinematix surveys 2,000 theatre-goers a week on buzz, reach and appeal for *upcoming* releases, forecasts first-day box office at 75–85% accuracy, and sells to "producers, distributors, studios, and exhibitors" ([Social Samosa](https://www.socialsamosa.com/industry-updates/ormax-media-rolls-out-pan-india-edition-film-tracking-forecasting-tool-9070349)); its partner list includes YRF, Dharma, Sony, Zee Studios, Jio Studios and "30+ studios and production houses" ([Ormax theatrical services](https://www.ormaxmedia.com/services/theatrical.html)). Everything Ormax sells is post-greenlight: it tracks a film that is already shot and announced. Nothing in the research sells a pre-greenlight, city-level "what is loved here but under-made" read, and funders' first question on any indie project is "who is the audience?" ([No Film School](https://nofilmschool.com/from-script-to-100k-equity-financing-navigating-the-greenlight-list)). AYTO's "greenlight pack" (lane with city-vs-country rank and supply count, titles, logline, three acts, references, composer shortlist, where it travels next) slots into the treatment-plus-deck that goes to a financier or OTT buyer, and one avoided flop or one *Court*-sized hit pays for the tool many times over. The honest caveat is that no interview or survey shows a production house would buy this; the case is assembled from failure rates, admitted uncertainty and existing Ormax spend, and sales to this segment are relationship-driven.

The other segments rank below on budget × urgency × path to money. Agencies come second: Tubular Labs contracts run $20,000–45,000 a year, averaging about $27,000 ([Vendr](https://vendr.com/buyer-guides/tubular-labs)); India's influencer-marketing industry is ₹3,000–3,500 crore ([The Week, Kofluence](https://www.theweek.in/wire-updates/business/2025/07/08/dcm19-kofluence.html)) growing 40–50% a year ([Qoruz](https://qoruz.com/agencies/top-influencer-marketing-agencies-india)); and JCDecaux has used Qloo since 2020 to plan hyper-localised out-of-home campaigns ([PR Newswire](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/qloo-and-jcdecaux-use-ai-powered-taste-data-to-deliver-hyper-localized-dooh-campaigns-302419224.html)), which makes a per-city creative brief the unbuilt other half of a workflow agencies already buy. Creators are the volume funnel, not the anchor: India has 4.12 million creators, 66% from non-metro markets ([ISB–Hashfame](https://www.isb.edu/news/press-releases/india-s-creator-economy-enters-a-new-phase-as-non-metro-creators-become-the-majority-reveals-isb-hashfame-report)), but Goldman counts only about 4% of the world's 50 million creators as professionals earning over $100k ([Goldman Sachs](https://www.goldmansachs.com/insights/articles/the-creator-economy-could-approach-half-a-trillion-dollars-by-2027)), half earn under $15,000 and 86% already use generative AI ([Spiralytics, Adobe 2025](https://spiralytics.com/blog/content-creator-statistics-2025)), and the tools they buy sit at $0–39 a month ([vidIQ plans](https://vidiq.com/plans)). Their documented pain is volume and burnout (46% of social media professionals at or near burnout, 73% saying they have lost motivation or creativity ([B&T, Metricool](https://www.bandt.com.au/things-have-intensified-social-media-experts-sound-burnout-alarm-as-report-finds-nearly-half-have-considered-quitting/))), not invisible local taste; AYTO solves the blank page for them, with the city angle as the differentiator rather than the core ask. Tiruppur is the best story and the weakest buyer: ~20,000 units, nine in ten TEA members MSMEs, and 100 units shipping half of exports ([Upstox](https://upstox.com/news/upstox-originals/investing/tiruppur-s-rise-how-one-textile-cluster-became-india-s-export-powerhouse/article-201077/)); a 50% US tariff has put more than 600,000 workers at risk ([BHRRC](https://www.business-humanrights.org/ar/latest-news/india-thousands-in-tiruppurs-textile-sector-out-of-work-as-tariffs-bring-industry-to-its-knees-wpftc/)); and a TIFAC gap analysis found the cluster "has largely neglected fashion forecasting and trend analysis" ([TIFAC](https://tifac.org.in/index.php/reports-publications/recent-publications/2-uncategorised/1077-readymade-garment-cluster-fashion-design-technology-gap-analysis-,-tirupur,-tamilnadu)). Design intent in Tiruppur mostly sits with the Western buyer's tech pack, and the budget to close the trend gap sits with the ~100 large exporters, so the realistic route is through AEPC or TEA programmes or a large exporter's design cell, not 20,000 units directly.

## No tool on the market starts from a place and writes the idea

The competitive field splits into three bands, none of which does what AYTO does. Demand and audience tools measure titles that already exist at country level: Parrot Analytics' unit is "Demand Expressions" per market, compared across markets per capita ([Parrot](https://tv.parrotanalytics.com/AR/?hl=en)); Ormax's national OTT report rests on 15,600 survey respondents ([afaqs](https://afaqs.com/news/media/indias-ott-audience-hits-601-million-paid-subscriptions-at-148-million-ormax-report-10469442)), a sample an academic critique calls "not robust for an audience study in India" ([Flow Journal](https://www.flowjournal.org/2024/04/how-to-measure-buzz/)). The film-AI cohort scores a script you already wrote: Cinelytic claims "85%+" forecast accuracy and offers "demographic profiling for US audience discovery" ([Cinelytic](https://www.cinelytic.com/)), with pricing reported to start around $10,000 a year ([Human Alternative, unverified](https://humanalternative.beehiiv.com/p/ai-vs-human-expertise-film-tv-scripting-success-technology)); Vault AI is the only vendor that names "market white space", and only at genre level with no city input ([Vault AI](https://www.vault-ai.com/)). Writing tools carry no audience data at all: Jasper's "Audiences" are typed personas with no external data source ([Jasper](https://www.jasper.ai/pricing)), Sudowrite is a fiction model at $10–44 a month ([Sudowrite](https://www.sudowrite.com/pricing)), and Final Draft 13 ships no generative AI ([Final Draft](https://www.finaldraft.com/blog/final-draft-13-is-here)). The closest "data → idea" products are single-platform and self-referential: Spotter Studio generates titles and thumbnails from a creator's own YouTube catalogue at $49 a month ([BusinessWire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20240903095771/en)) and vidIQ sells "fresh content ideas backed by data" from YouTube keywords ([vidIQ](https://vidiq.com/plans)); both answer "what should this channel make next", never "what does this city want that nobody is making". Free trend tools cannot reach the question either: Google Trends offers city granularity for a search term but metro-level only in the US ([Google News Initiative](https://newsinitiative.withgoogle.com/vi/resources/trainings/data-journalism/google-trends-improving-search-results)), and Pinterest and TikTok trend centres stop at region or country.

Qloo is the only data source with the primitives, and it does not sell a creative tool. Its Insights API takes `signal.location` as a point, polygon or locality, filters by geohash or metro, and returns heatmaps with city or neighbourhood boundaries and separate affinity and popularity fields ([Insights API deep dive](https://docs.qloo.com/reference/insights-api-deep-dive)); its homepage claims 600M+ entities and locality intelligence "from a block to a country" with precision "down to hundreds of feet", and its only call to action is "Book a Demo" ([Qloo](https://www.qloo.com/)). Qloo's own lift framing is "local lift vs. the US baseline" ([Qloo location solutions](https://www.qloo.com/solutions/location-geographic-analysis)), so a city-over-its-own-country lift for India is a computation nobody ships. No public API price exists anywhere ([Capterra](https://www.capterra.com/p/10013772/Qloo/)), its $77M of funding was earmarked partly for "a self-service insights platform" ([Fortune](https://www.fortune.com/2024/07/02/leonardo-dicaprio-qloo-elton-john-netflix-starbucks-ticketmaster-a-i-bobby-sharma-nba)), and Gartner named it one of two "Set Diamonds" among 33 synthetic-population vendors in 2026 ([PR Newswire](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/qloo-recognized-in-the-set-diamonds-category-in-the-2026-gartner-emerging-tech-ai-to-watch-in-synthetic-popular-simulation-research-302878163.html)). Qloo's named customers use it for precisely AYTO's decision shape, "decide what to put where": Netflix shifted *Bridgerton* merchandise from tea to coffee for US audiences ([Qloo blog](https://www.qloo.com/blog/netflix-and-Qloo-elevate-merchandising-with-ai-driven-audience-insights/)), Starbucks tailors in-store playlists to neighbourhoods and Hershey's customises assorted bags ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qloo)), and an unnamed film studio learned its film would appeal to electronic-music and anime fans and saw a double-digit lift in trailer view-through ([AdExchanger](https://www.adexchanger.com/ai/qloo-snags-25-million-in-series-c-funding-to-unravel-the-mystery-of-consumer-tastes/)). The pitch line writes itself: AYTO is the self-serve version of what Qloo already sells to Netflix and Starbucks, pointed at the person who has not made the thing yet.

The positioning sentence the research supports is: **AYTO is the only tool that starts from a place and a format, reads what that city loves more than its own country across films, series, music, places and brands, scores which of those flavours are under-made, and writes the idea into that gap, at a creator price rather than a studio price.** What is genuinely new is the chain, not the data; the honest framing is "built on Qloo's taste graph; the lift-vs-country, the gap score and the writing are ours". Three weaknesses deserve a sentence each on the submission page. Qloo's depth in Indian tier-2 cities and its affinity normalisation are undocumented, so the "Hyderabad > India" claim should be shown with live outputs rather than asserted; your own verified runs (Hyderabad-only crime films such as *V*, *Vettaiyan*, *Maestro*; composers Devi Sri Prasad, Anirudh, Sreerama Chandra, recorded in `PLAN.md` and `README.md`) are that evidence. Hackathon keys die after judging and commercial pricing is unpublished, which is a real go-to-market risk that rivals with public pricing (Chartmetric, Exolyt, Foursquare) do not carry. And the tension between a ₹10–200 Cr buyer and a "creator price" is resolved by a two-tier model, a studio seat priced against Ormax and Cinelytic and a self-serve creator tier priced against vidIQ, rather than by choosing one.

## Five extensions deepen the engine; five neighbours are different products

The 2026 field has converged on three motifs that are now table stakes: receipts and grounding validators (TasteRouter "rejects names not present in that run's Qloo results" ([taste-router](https://github.com/happy520ai/taste-router)); Open Slot ships "evidence cards, not a score" ([open-slot](https://github.com/guptachetan1995/open-slot))), human-in-the-loop approvals (Open Slot, TasteCase ([tastecase](https://github.com/CryptoZephyr/tastecase))), and an MCP surface (TasteRouter, Taste Match ([qloo-mcp](https://github.com/NoBanks/qloo-mcp)), Qloo Workbench). AYTO's "every name traces to a Qloo request" and its MCP door are therefore necessary but not differentiating. What nobody in the found set does is compute a city-versus-country lift and write the creative *into* the gap. TasteRouter, the closest neighbour, answers "where does my idea fit" with a Taste-Fit Score built from heatmap affinity, venue overlap and category saturation, and explicitly disclaims "rent, zoning, or foot traffic" ([taste-router](https://github.com/happy520ai/taste-router)); AYTO answers the inverse, "what should I make here". That inversion is the clearest sentence to put on the Devpost page.

The extension ideas sort by whether they stay inside AYTO's three inputs (place, format, the user's LLM) and add one Qloo primitive the field is not using. The strongest is a **casting and cameo localiser**: a run of `/v2/insights` with `filter.type=urn:entity:person`, the city as `signal.location.query`, the genre as a tag filter and `feature.explainability=true`, differenced against the country run, so a Hyderabad crime short gets actors, hosts and comedians who over-index locally. It is one new entity type in the existing engine, Netflix pays Qloo for exactly this read on person entities, and it puts a Qloo-sourced name with an explainability score on screen for every cast suggestion. The second is a **two-city port** ("make my Hyderabad idea work in Las Vegas") on `GET /v2/analysis/compare`, which takes two groups of entities, an optional `filter.location.query` and a `filter.subtype` of `urn:tag:genre` ([Analysis Compare](https://docs.qloo.com/reference/analysis-compare-1)); it reuses AYTO's existing diff and edit-and-rerun views, touches an endpoint almost no entry uses, and is the most "agentic-feeling" demo, but its response shape is only partly documented. The third is a **multi-city ad-creative localiser**, the existing run fanned out across N cities into a table, which has the clearest paying buyer (JCDecaux-adjacent agencies) but adds little new Qloo surface. The fourth, a **creative-brief QA** that resolves every named reference in a script via `/search` and scores its local affinity, is the strongest reverse mode and would let AYTO claim both directions, but free-text entity resolution is the riskiest thing to attempt in two days. A **gap watch** on `/v2/trending`'s weekly `population_rank_velocity` ([Get Trending](https://docs.qloo.com/reference/get-trending)) is the only idea that is agentic in the "runs without you" sense, but your README records that trending is a frozen weekly snapshot in the hackathon data, so it cannot be demonstrated live before judging and should be described as "next", not built.

|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Casting & cameo localiser | `filter.type=urn:entity:person` + explainability, city vs country | Short-film / series maker | Low | No | **Build if any time remains** |
| Two-city port | `/v2/analysis/compare`, `filter.subtype=urn:tag:genre` | Creator moving an idea | Medium | No | Next release |

Five neighbours should be named as "not us" on the submission page, because each is already occupied and naming them shows judges you know the field. Site selection belongs to TasteRouter and TasteOps ([TasteOps](https://github.com/maitray-agrawal/TasteOps)) and to Qloo's own Clarion Partners case, which used location-level affinity to persuade a retailer to lease on Chicago's Magnificent Mile ([Qloo Clarion story](https://www.qloo.com/resources/client-stories/clarion-magnificent-mile)). Venue booking is Open Slot; tour routing is Advance ([advance](https://github.com/JoeProAI/advance)), Opener and TasteRouter's tour mode; sponsor matching is SponsorMatch ([sponsormatch](https://github.com/alexespinoza28/sponsormatch)); cultural due diligence won in 2025 as Alloy ([alloy](https://github.com/MR-GREEN1337/alloy)). The crowded consumer cluster (weekend concierges, group-dinner negotiators, gift finders, two-person taste overlap) is exactly the list third-party guides recommend ([The GenAI Magazine](https://thegenaimagazine.com/qloo-agentic-hackathon-2026-prizes-deadline-ideas)) and none of it placed in 2025. One judge-specific note: Live Nation is both a Qloo customer and the employer of judge Michael Abrams, so AYTO's composer shortlist and Campaign Soundtrack will be read by someone who knows the data; keep those cards tight and sourced rather than cutting them.

## Five hard requirements decide eligibility; Qloo depth decides ties

The official rules, fetched directly, set the submission period as 30 September 9:00 am ET to **30 October 2026 11:45 pm ET**, judging from 2 November 12:00 pm ET to 5 November 11:45 pm ET, and winners "on or around November 5, 2026 (3:00 pm Eastern Time)" ([Official Rules §1](https://qloo.devpost.com/rules)). Because US daylight time ends on 1 November, the deadline is EDT and the judging window is EST: the deadline is **Sat 31 Oct 03:45 UTC = 09:15 IST**, judging opens Mon 2 Nov 22:30 IST, and the finale lands Fri 6 Nov 01:30 IST. Third-party coverage that puts judging through 16 November and winners on 23 November is wrong; the rules say the Official Rules prevail over other materials ([Official Rules §11](https://qloo.devpost.com/rules)). The top three finalists "will appear on air with Jason Calacanis, Cedric the Entertainer, and Alex Elias, who will pick the winner", must be available for preparation beforehand, and will be notified "very soon after the Hackathon closes" ([Overview](https://qloo.devpost.com/)); decide the presenter now and watch the Devpost contact email from 31 October.

Eligibility is five items, each disqualifying on its own. The overview requires "a link to a functional demo application" judges can try end to end, a public GitHub/GitLab/Bitbucket repo with "all source code, assets, and instructions needed for the project to run", a text description that explains "what makes it 'Qloo-powered'", external hosting ("Submissions that only run locally or rely on private access will not qualify"), and "an open-source license, with the file visible in About section of your repo"; it adds, in capitals, "Demo videos are not required" ([Overview](https://qloo.devpost.com/)). The project itself must integrate the Qloo API and either function as an agentic tool, be built on an agent framework, or wire Qloo into an existing agent; it "must function as depicted in the video and/or expressed in the text description", and a project that predates 30 September must have had Qloo integrated after that date with an explanation of how it was "significantly updated" ([Official Rules §4](https://qloo.devpost.com/rules)). The hackathon manager clarified on 26 October that "API requests are free through judging" and "Judges must be able to use the submission as a live tool/app" ([Devpost forum](https://qloo.devpost.com/forum_topics/45690-tasteatlas-no-cost-judging-access-and-cached-demo-requirements)), which, read with the rule that judges "may choose to judge based solely on the text description, images, and video", means the safe configuration is a public URL with no sign-up and a short optional screencast as insurance against downtime. Keys work only on `https://hackathon.api.qloo.com`, are deactivated after the hackathon closes, may be cached server-side "with no time limit", and response data must not be stored in a public repo ([Developer guide](https://docs.qloo.com/reference/qloo-llm-hackathon-developer-guide)); the kit adds "Never ... put one in source control, a browser app, a public MCP configuration, or a demo recording" ([API_ACCESS.md](https://github.com/qloo/qloo-hackathon-kit/blob/main/docs/API_ACCESS.md)).

Scoring is two stages. Stage one is a pass/fail check that the project "reasonably fits the theme and reasonably applies the required APIs"; stage two is four equally weighted criteria, quoted verbatim: "Technological Implementation: How thoroughly and skillfully does the project use Qloo? Does the code reflect genuine effort and a working, non-trivial implementation?"; "Design: Does the project deliver a complete, coherent product experience, not just a technical proof of concept?"; "Potential Impact: Does the project make a credible, specific case for solving a real problem for a real audience and does the solution actually address that problem based on what's demonstrated?"; "Quality of the Idea: Is this a creative, non-obvious use of Qloo and does the team demonstrate genuine understanding of the problem space they're working in?" Ties break on the first criterion, then the second, and judging "may utilize expert panels, peer review, automated AI-driven analysis, or any combination" ([Official Rules §6](https://qloo.devpost.com/rules)). The organisers' own framing is the test AYTO already built a card for: "If your submission would work the same without Qloo, you're building the wrong thing", and "We're not looking for demos of what LLMs can already do" ([Overview](https://qloo.devpost.com/)). Qloo's kit says projects "are stronger when they use Qloo results as evidence in a clear product flow rather than showing an isolated API response" and asks for a problem statement, the workflow or MCP tools used, "a redacted request-to-result explanation, including any entity/tag choices", screenshots with no credentials, clean-environment setup steps, and "known limitations, including what the result does not establish" ([SUBMISSION.md](https://github.com/qloo/qloo-hackathon-kit/blob/main/docs/SUBMISSION.md)); its safe-use page asks you to "distinguish a Qloo result from your own product interpretation, and do not describe an affinity as a causal claim" ([SAFE_USE.md](https://github.com/qloo/qloo-hackathon-kit/blob/main/docs/SAFE_USE.md)), which AYTO's "built on what this city already loves, never a hit" language already satisfies.

The scale of the field sets the bar for first impressions. 2,718 participants had registered by 28 October ([Overview](https://qloo.devpost.com/)); the 2025 edition drew 2,848–2,893 and produced 344 gallery projects, of which four won ([2025 gallery](https://qloo-hackathon.devpost.com/project-gallery)), so expect roughly 300–350 submissions competing for three prizes of $15,000, $6,000 and $4,000, plus Calacanis's personal "$25,000 into his favorite entry" with no published terms ([Overview](https://qloo.devpost.com/)). The common ways to lose are mundane: Devpost says "many submissions are disqualified for failing baseline requirements" ([Devpost blog](https://info.devpost.com/blog/understanding-hackathon-submission-and-judging-criteria)); eight of twelve forum threads are API-key problems, including an unanswered 401 "API keys mismatch" on the correct host ([Devpost forum](https://qloo.devpost.com/forum_topics/45602-hackathon-key-returns-401-api-keys-mismatch)); and a panel of five experienced judges flagged "so back-end heavy there's almost no front end", "Ambiguity is a red flag", polished homepages over thin code, and failing to "nail the prompt" ([Devpost judging tips](https://info.devpost.com/blog/hackathon-judging-tips)). An MLH coach who judged about twenty online hackathons warns that judges have "around five minutes" per project and that "if they can't find key information about your project, they're going to have to move onto the next one" ([Jacklyn Biggin](https://dev.to/jacklyn/how-to-make-your-devpost-submissions-not-suck-37i0)). Editing a published submission sends it "back to the hackathon managers' review queue" ([Devpost help](https://help.devpost.com/article/81-my-submission-disappeared)), so freeze edits at least three hours before 09:15 IST on 31 October.

Two adjustments to `PLAN.md`'s cut order are worth making. The plan cuts the with/without diff fourth, after the MCP door, the fashion format and the travels map; the research says the diff is the single most direct piece of evidence for the organisers' stated test and for the tie-break criterion, so it should be uncuttable, with the cut order becoming MCP door → travels map → fashion format → Ask box. And the three-audience framing should give way to one lead audience on the submission page, while the three showcases stay as proof that one engine serves three formats.

A strong submission, judged by what placed in 2025, is legible in a minute and honest about its edges. All four 2025 winners shipped a live URL on a cheap PaaS plus a public repo, named their agent architecture, and gave a dedicated section to how Qloo was used: GeoTaste, the $10,000 winner from a team of two, led with "GeoTaste transforms location into business insights" and a complete product of map, 13 chart types and chat ([GeoTaste on Devpost](https://devpost.com/software/geotaste-your-agentic-qloo-taste-business-consultant)); Alloy, a solo honourable mention, wrote a section titled "Our Unfair Advantage: A Deeper, Smarter Qloo Integration" about resolving "cultural proxies" through `/search`, admitted the Persona API "is a production-level feature not available in the hackathon", and still placed ([Alloy on Devpost](https://devpost.com/software/axiom-2bn391)); Resonance AI said outright "I couldn't build a custom frontend" and shipped on Streamlit ([Resonance AI on Devpost](https://devpost.com/software/resonance-ai-1hjbf0)). Business and market-intelligence framings took the top prize and an honourable mention in front of a panel of investors, and the 2026 panel is again half investors and executives (Calacanis, Boehly, Abrams, Seligman) ([Overview](https://qloo.devpost.com/)), so the Devpost story should carry a short "who pays and why now" paragraph drawn from the producer evidence above. Devpost's own guidance is to open by stating what the app does "within the first few seconds", keep any video under about three minutes, show "real input", "skip slide decks", and avoid "a generic AI-written script" ([Devpost video tips](https://info.devpost.com/blog/6-tips-for-making-a-hackathon-demo-video)); judges say "a nice video with a weak description doesn't work" and that code "should match the polish of the presentation" because they "may dig into GitHub" ([Devpost judging tips](https://info.devpost.com/blog/hackathon-judging-tips)). Because the rules allow "automated AI-driven analysis", the description should be keyword-explicit rather than purely narrative: name the endpoints (`/search`, `/v2/tags`, `/v2/insights` with `signal.location.query`, `filter.tags`, `filter.type=urn:tag`, `urn:heatmap`), the agent surface (MCP over Streamable HTTP), the problem, and the audience in plain words. The sixty-second version for the 5 November finale is one city, one theme, one lane with its city-versus-country rank and supply count, one written film, one receipt opened on screen, and one glance at the with/without card.

## Conclusion

The research reframes AYTO from "a tool for creators" into "a pre-greenlight instrument for a market that just proved budgets cannot buy hits", with creators as the free tier that proves the engine. That reframing matters commercially, because the buyer with money and urgency is the one whose incumbent (Ormax) only starts working after the decision AYTO informs, and it matters for the hackathon, because an investor-heavy panel scored the two business-intelligence entries highest in 2025 and the 2026 criteria ask for one credible audience rather than three plausible ones. The deepest insight from the competitive scan is that AYTO's moat is not Qloo data, which TasteRouter, Open Slot and a dozen MCP wrappers also reach, but the direction of the arrow: everyone else routes an idea to a place, and AYTO routes a place to an idea, then proves every noun.

What this implies for the last two days is restraint. The engine, the receipts, the grounding check, the with/without card and the MCP door already exist; the three things that still decide whether AYTO is judged at all (a public URL, a visible licence, a submitted page) are hours of work, not days, and every hour spent on a new format or endpoint before those three are green is an hour that risks the whole entry. Once they are green, the one addition worth making is the casting localiser, because it adds a new Qloo entity type with explainability on a card an actor-judge will understand; the two-city port, the multi-city localiser and the gap watch are the roadmap to put under "What's next" and to carry into the Calacanis conversation if the finale call comes.
